# F2: quantum-side classification theorems

## Findings

### [1] Murray–von Neumann (1936–1943) reduced von Neumann algebra classification to factors via an essentially unique direct-integral decomposition, and proved every factor is type I, II, or III.
- quote: "every von Neumann algebra on a separable Hilbert space is isomorphic to a direct integral of factors. This decomposition is essentially unique. Thus, the problem of classifying isomorphism classes of von Neumann algebras on separable Hilbert spaces can be reduced to that of classifying isomorphism classes of factors. Murray & von Neumann (1936) showed that every factor has one of 3 types"
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision 1380108868; cites Murray & von Neumann 1936, 1937, 1943; von Neumann 1949)
- confidence: high

### [2] The amenable (hyperfinite/AFD/injective) factors are completely classified: unique factors of types I_n, I_∞, II_1, II_∞ and III_λ for 0<λ≤1, with type III_0 corresponding to ergodic flows — Murray–von Neumann (1943) for I and II_1, Connes (1976) for the rest (III_1 completed by Haagerup).
- quote: "The amenable factors have been classified: there is a unique one of each of the types I_n, I_∞, II_1, II_∞, III_λ, for 0 < λ ≤ 1, and the ones of type III_0 correspond to certain ergodic flows. ... The ones of type I and II_1 were classified by Murray & von Neumann (1943), and the remaining ones were classified by Connes (1976), except for the type III1 case which was completed by Haagerup."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision; underlying results: Connes, "Classification of injective factors", Annals of Mathematics 104(1):73–115, 1976)
- confidence: high

### [3] Outside the amenable case no CFSG-style classification exists: McDuff (1969) produced an uncountable family of non-isomorphic separable type II_1 factors, and type III factors are stratified only coarsely by the Connes spectrum (III_0, III_λ for 0<λ<1, III_1).
- quote: "McDuff (1969) found an uncountable family of such groups with non-isomorphic group von Neumann algebras, thus showing the existence of uncountably many different separable type II_1 factors."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision; underlying: McDuff 1969)
- confidence: high

### [4] Factor invariants beyond type are only partially classified: Connes proved the fundamental group of the II_1 factor of SL(3,Z) (a property (T) group) is countable, and Popa later constructed factors with trivial fundamental group (e.g. from Z^2 ⋊ SL(2,Z)).
- quote: "Connes then showed that the von Neumann group algebra of a countable discrete group with Kazhdan's property (T) ... such as SL(3,Z), has a countable fundamental group. Subsequently, Sorin Popa showed that the fundamental group can be trivial for certain groups, including the semidirect product of Z^2 by SL(2,Z)."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision)
- confidence: high

### [5] Gelfand–Naimark (1943) is a representation theorem, not a classification: every C*-algebra is *-isomorphic to a norm-closed adjoint-closed subalgebra of B(H); only the commutative case is fully classified (Gelfand representation A ≅ C_0(X), X the character space).
- quote: "In fact, every C*-algebra, A, is *-isomorphic to a norm-closed adjoint closed subalgebra of B(H) for a suitable Hilbert space, H; this is the content of the Gelfand–Naimark theorem."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision 1375015578; Gelfand–Naimark 1943)
- confidence: high

### [6] Finite-dimensional C*-algebras are completely classified (Artin–Wedderburn type): A ≅ ⊕_{e∈min A} A_e with A_e ≅ M(dim e, C), and the dimension vector {dim(e)} uniquely determines the isomorphism class.
- quote: "A finite-dimensional C*-algebra, A, is canonically isomorphic to a finite direct sum ... Each C*-algebra, Ae, is isomorphic (in a noncanonical way) to the full matrix algebra M(dim(e), C). The finite family indexed on min A given by {dim(e)}e is called the dimension vector of A. This vector uniquely determines the isomorphism class of a finite-dimensional C*-algebra."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision)
- confidence: high

### [7] The Elliott-style classification of separable simple nuclear C*-algebras is an active, unfinished program — explicitly described as research "to obtain classification, or to determine the extent of which classification is possible"; landmark partials include Connes 1976 (injective factors, Annals of Math. 104:73–115) and Rørdam 2002 ("Classification of nuclear simple C*-algebras", EMS Encyclopaedia Math. Sci. 126).
- quote: "Another active area of research is the program to obtain classification, or to determine the extent of which classification is possible, for separable simple nuclear C*-algebras."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebra
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision; Rørdam reference 2002)
- confidence: medium

### [8] Stinespring's dilation theorem (1955) classifies completely positive maps as compressions of *-representations (Φ(a)=V*π(a)V), and minimal Stinespring representations are unique up to unitary equivalence — this uniqueness is what underwrites classification results for special classes of quantum channels.
- quote: "The converse of the theorem is true trivially. So Stinespring's result classifies completely positive maps. ... In particular, if both Stinespring representations are minimal, W is unitary. Thus minimal Stinespring representations are unique up to a unitary transformation. ... For example, the uniqueness part of the theorem has been used to classify certain classes of quantum channels."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stinespring_dilation_theorem
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025-03 (page revision 1345142488; Stinespring, Proc. AMS 6:211–216, 1955)
- confidence: high

### [9] In finite dimension, Choi's theorem (1975) gives every CP map Φ: C^{n×n} → C^{m×m} a Kraus form Φ(A)=Σ_{i=1}^{N} K_i A K_i* with N ≤ nm; the minimal such N (Kraus rank) is an invariant (rank 1 = "pure" channel), but this is a normal form, not a full isomorphism classification of quantum channels.
- quote: "By Choi's theorem on completely positive maps, Ψ must take the form Ψ(A)=Σ_{i=1}^{N} K_i A K_i* where N ≤ nm. The matrices Ki are called Kraus operators of Ψ ... The minimum number of Kraus operators is called the Kraus rank of Ψ. A channel with Kraus rank 1 is called pure."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_channel
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025 (page revision 1369381836; Choi 1975, Linear Algebra Appl. 10:285–290)
- confidence: high

### [10] Doplicher–Roberts reconstruction (1989) fully classifies DHR superselection data: any symmetric tensor *-category with conjugates, direct sums, subobjects and End(1)=C is equivalent to finite-dimensional unitary representations of a compact group (or supergroup) unique up to isomorphism.
- quote: "every symmetric tensor star-category with conjugates, direct sums, subobjects and endomorphism ring of the tensor unit isomorphic to the complex numbers, End(1)=C, is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional unitary linear representations of a compact supergroup, which is unique up to isomorphism."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Doplicher-Roberts+reconstruction+theorem
- source_type: community
- published: page last revised 2026-05-09; primary: Doplicher & Roberts, "A new duality theory for compact groups", Inventiones mathematicae 98(1):157–218, 1989
- confidence: high

### [11] Deligne's theorem (2002) classifies characteristic-zero symmetric tensor categories with subexponential growth as representation categories of supergroups, i.e. supercommutative superalgebra is forced by full Tannaka duality in this setting.
- quote: "Deligne's theorem on tensor categories establishes Tannaka duality between linear tensor categories in characteristic zero subject to just a mild size constraint (subexponential growth ...), supergroups ("supersymmetries"), realizing these tensor categories as categories of representations of these supergroups."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Deligne%27s+theorem+on+tensor+categories
- source_type: community
- published: page retrieved 2026-10-29; primary: Deligne 2002 ("Catégories tensorielles", Moscow Math. J. 2002)
- confidence: high

### [12] Fusion categories (rigid semisimple k-linear monoidal, finitely many simples, End(1)=k) are not fully classified: every fusion category is Rep of some weak Hopf algebra (Ostrik), and the Etingof–Nikshych–Ostrik conjecture that every fusion category admits a pivotal structure (Annals of Math. 162:581–642, 2005) remains an open structural question.
- quote: "Under Tannaka duality, every fusion category C arises as the representation category of a weak Hopf algebra (Ostrik). However, this does not mean that every fusion category admits a fiber functor to the category of vector spaces ... Conjecture (Etingof, Nikshych, and Ostrik): Every fusion category admits a pivotal structure."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/fusion+category
- source_type: community
- published: page retrieved 2026-10-29; primary: Etingof, Nikshych, Ostrik, "On fusion categories", Annals of Mathematics 162(2):581–642, 2005
- confidence: medium

## Suggested follow-ups
- Elliott classification program current status: UCT conjecture, Kirchberg–Phillips classification of purely infinite simple nuclear C*-algebras, and what remains open for stably finite algebras (needs primary survey, e.g. Elliott–Toms or Rørdam's 2002 chapter).
- Jones' subfactors: index set {4cos²(π/n) : n≥3} ∪ [4,∞] and ADE classification of subfactors of index < 4 — an adjacent fully-classified island not covered in this angle.
- Low-dimensional quantum channels: whether one-qubit (d=2) channels admit canonical forms up to unitary conjugation / local-unitary equivalence, as the strongest known "classification" fragment on the channel side.
