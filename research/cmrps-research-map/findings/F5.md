# F5: categorical and reconstruction approaches

## Findings

### [1] Baez and Dolan's 1995 cobordism hypothesis originates the classification program for extended TQFTs by proposing that n-dimensional unitary extended TQFTs are weak n-functors from the free stable weak n-category with duals on one object to the n-category of n-Hilbert spaces.
- quote: "We then propose a description of n-dimensional unitary extended TQFTs as weak n-functors from the 'free stable weak n-category with duals on one object' to the n-category of 'n-Hilbert spaces'."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/q-alg/9503002
- source_type: primary
- published: 1995-03-05 (v1); J. Math. Phys. 36 (1995) 6073-6105
- confidence: high

### [2] Lurie's 2009 "On the Classification of Topological Field Theories" (111 pages, report number AIM 2009-24) supplies an informal proof sketch of the Baez-Dolan cobordism hypothesis, which classifies extended topological quantum field theories.
- quote: "This paper provides an informal sketch of a proof of the Baez-Dolan cobordism hypothesis, which provides a classification for extended topological quantum field theories."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.0465
- source_type: primary
- published: 2009-05-04
- confidence: high

### [3] The functorial TQFT axioms are due to Atiyah (Publ. Math. IHES 68 (1988) 175-186, following Segal's 1988 2D CFT axiomatization), and nLab stresses that this axiomatization is "notorious[ly]" in contrast to the situation for general non-topological QFTs, where no comparable axioms exist.
- quote: "With the mathematical axiomatization of TQFTs both established and tractable (Atiyah 1989, following Segal 1988's axiomatization of 2D CFT) — this in notorious contrast to the situation for general non-topological QFTs"
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/topological+quantum+field+theory
- source_type: community
- published: page last revised 2026-10-06; cites Atiyah 1988/1989 and Segal 1988
- confidence: high

### [4] Witten's 1988 papers originated topological quantum field theory physically (cohomological QFT from string theory, Comm. Math. Phys. 117 (1988) 353-386; Chern-Simons/Jones polynomial, Comm. Math. Phys. 121 (1989) 351-399), and TQFTs now serve as standard tools in low-dimensional topology.
- quote: "The concept originates in the guise of cohomological quantum field theory motivated from TQFTs appearing in string theory in Edward Witten: Topological quantum field theory, Comm. Math. Phys. 117 3 (1988) 353-386"
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/topological+quantum+field+theory
- source_type: community
- published: 1988/1989 (originals); nLab page revised 2026-10-06
- confidence: high

### [5] Butterfield and Isham (1998) proved the Kochen-Specker theorem equivalent to the statement that a certain presheaf on the category of self-adjoint operators has no global elements, and used topos theory of presheaves to define generalized valuations whose truth values are contextual, multi-valued sieves.
- quote: "The first step in our theory is to demonstrate that the Kochen-Specker theorem is equivalent to the statement that a certain presheaf defined on the category of self-adjoint operators has no global elements. ... the space of contexts and the multi-valued logic for each context come naturally from the topos theory of presheaves."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9803055
- source_type: primary
- published: 1998-03-20 (v1), 1998-10-13 (v4)
- confidence: high

### [6] The Isham-Döring topos program (2007-2008) claims that constructing a theory of physics is equivalent to finding a representation in a topos of a formal language attached to the system, with classical physics recovered when the topos is the category of sets (published J. Math. Phys. 49:053515, 2008).
- quote: "Our basic contention is that constructing a theory of physics is equivalent to finding a representation in a topos of a certain formal language that is attached to the system. Classical physics arises when the topos is the category of sets."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0703060
- source_type: primary
- published: 2007-03-07; J. Math. Phys. 49:053515 (2008)
- confidence: high

### [7] Isham's 2010 review "Topos Methods in the Foundations of Physics" (24 pages, in Deep Beauty, ed. Halvorson, Cambridge University Press) is the canonical conceptual introduction to the topos approach to formulating physical theories.
- quote: "This article gives a conceptual introduction to the topos approach to the formulation of physical theories."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3564
- source_type: primary
- published: 2010-04-20
- confidence: high

### [8] A Yoneda lemma for categorical supermaps was established in 2026 (Wilson, Hefford, Hoffreumon): whenever a physical theory has a suitable channel-state duality, categorical supermaps on that theory are concretely representable through that duality, with applications to Boxworld and real quantum theory.
- quote: "we establish the Yoneda lemma for categorical supermaps, which states that whenever a physical theory has a suitable notion of channel-state duality, then categorical supermaps on that theory can be concretely represented in terms of that duality."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23865
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02-27 (v1), 2026-06-21 (v3)
- confidence: high

### [9] Hefford and Wilson (2024) give a profunctorial semantics for quantum supermaps in which morphisms of strong profunctors categorify supermaps, and they identify the Yoneda lemma and representability as the mechanism behind the resulting factorisation theorems.
- quote: "We demonstrate that at the heart of these factorisation theorems lies the Yoneda lemma and the notion of representability."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02997
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05 (v1), 2024-05-23 (v2); DOI 10.1145/3661814.3662123
- confidence: high

### [10] The Gelfand-Naimark theorem (1943) states that every C*-algebra is isomorphic to a C*-algebra of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space, reconstructing Hilbert-space quantum mechanics from observable algebras (original: Gelfand and Neumark, "On the imbedding of normed rings into the ring of operators in Hilbert space", Recueil Mathematique 12(54):2 (1943) 197-217).
- quote: "The Gelfand-Neumark theorem ... says that every C*-algebra is isomorphic to a C*-algebra of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Gelfand-Naimark+theorem
- source_type: community
- published: original 1943; nLab page revised 2024-02-24
- confidence: high

### [11] Gelfand duality reconstructs a classical state space from its observables: every commutative C*-algebra A is equivalent to the continuous functions on its Gelfand spectrum sp(A), and unital commutative C*-algebras are contravariantly equivalent to compact Hausdorff spaces, the basis for treating noncommutative C*-algebras as formal duals of noncommutative spaces.
- quote: "Every (nonunital) commutative C*-algebra A is equivalent to the C*-algebra of continuous functions on the topological space called its Gelfand spectrum sp(A). This theorem is the basis for regarding non-commutative C*-algebras as formal duals to spaces in noncommutative geometry."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Gelfand+duality
- source_type: community
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [12] Stone representation is an equivalence of categories Stone^op -> Bool: the Boolean algebra of a Stone space is its algebra of clopen sets, so a Boolean algebra of classical propositions completely reconstructs its spectrum of valuations.
- quote: "The representable functor restricts to an equivalence of categories Stone^op -> Bool. ... The Boolean algebra corresponding to a Stone space consists of its clopen sets."
- url: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Stone+duality
- source_type: community
- published: unknown
- confidence: high
