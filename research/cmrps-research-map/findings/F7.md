# F7: obstructions and non-classifiability

## Findings

### [1] The Adyan–Rabin theorem (Adyan 1955; Rabin 1958) makes every Markov property of finitely presentable groups algorithmically undecidable, so no uniform recognition/classification procedure can exist for such properties.
- quote: "Let P be a Markov property of finitely presentable groups. Then there does not exist an algorithm that, given a finite presentation G = ⟨ X | R ⟩, decides whether or not the group G defined by this presentation has property P."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adian%E2%80%93Rabin_theorem
- source_type: secondary
- published: 1955 (Adyan, Doklady AN SSSR 103:533-535); 1958 (Rabin, Ann. of Math. 67:172-194); page rev. 2026-08-17
- confidence: high

### [2] Markov-and-hence-undecidable properties include finiteness, simplicity, freeness, abelianness, solvability, amenability, word-hyperbolicity, torsion-freeness, residual finiteness, and finite cohomological dimension — i.e., even deciding whether a finitely presented group is finite or trivial is impossible.
- quote: "The following properties of finitely presented groups are Markov and therefore are algorithmically undecidable by the Adyan–Rabin theorem: 1. Being the trivial group. 2. Being a finite group. ... 15. Being a simple group."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adian%E2%80%93Rabin_theorem
- source_type: secondary
- published: 1955/1958 results; list as compiled on page rev. 2026-08-17
- confidence: high

### [3] Undecidability extends beyond Markov properties: Collins (1969) proved the Hopfian property undecidable for finitely presentable groups even though neither being Hopfian nor being non-Hopfian is a Markov property.
- quote: "Collins (1969) proved that the property of being Hopfian is undecidable for finitely presentable groups, while neither being Hopfian nor being non-Hopfian are Markov."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adian%E2%80%93Rabin_theorem
- source_type: secondary
- published: 1969 (Collins, Archiv der Mathematik 20:235-240)
- confidence: high

### [4] Markov's undecidability of the homeomorphism problem for 4-manifolds has been made explicit and low-complexity: Tancer (2023, rev. 2025) shows #_9(S^2 x S^2) is algorithmically unrecognizable, improving Gordon's #_12(S^2 x S^2), using the Adian–Rabin set construction.
- quote: "Markov proved that there exists an unrecognizable 4-manifold, that is, a 4-manifold for which the homeomorphism problem is undecidable. ... our approach allows to show that the connected sum #_9(S^2 x S^2) is unrecognizable while the previous best result is the unrecognizability of #_12(S^2 x S^2) due to Gordon."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07421
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-10-11 (v1); 2025-02-21 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [5] The tame-wild dichotomy holds even for derived categories: Bekkert and Drozd (2003) prove every finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field is derived tame or derived wild, via matrix problems (boxes and reduction algorithm), and wildness is degeneration-robust.
- quote: "We prove that every finite dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field is either derived tame or derived wild. The proof is based on the technique of matrix problems (boxes and reduction algorithm). It implies, in particular, that any degeneration of a derived wild algebra is derived wild."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0310352
- source_type: primary
- published: 2003-10-22
- confidence: high

### [6] Wildness is a genuine non-classifiability theorem, not just a bad-complexity signal: Buchweitz and Leuschke (2005; Amer. J. Math.) show the Cohen-Macaulay representation theory of the generic determinant is "quite wild" — already in rank two the isomorphism classes cannot be parametrized by a finite-dimensional variety over the coefficients.
- quote: "The classification shows not only that the Cohen-Macaulay representation theory of the generic determinant is wild in the tame-wild dichotomy, but that it is quite wild: even in rank two, the isomorphism classes cannot be parametrized by a finite-dimensional variety over the coefficients."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505315
- source_type: primary
- published: 2005-05-15 (v1); 2006-04-17 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [7] The tame-wild dichotomy is a concrete tractability boundary outside representation theory: Grochow and Qiao (2015) give polynomial-time group isomorphism (GpI) for "tame" extensions (Sylow p-subgroup cyclic, dihedral, semi-dihedral, or generalized quaternion) and show the needed representation/cohomology bounds fail in the wild case.
- quote: "This suggests a precise sense in which the tame-wild dichotomy from representation theory may also be a dividing line between the (currently) easy and hard instances of GpI."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01917
- source_type: primary
- published: 2015-07-07 (v1); 2015-07-09 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [8] Wildness yields explicit non-classifiability in equivariant homotopy theory: Grevstad and May (2025, rev. 2026) prove the cohomological Mackey algebra is derived wild whenever G surjects onto a p-group of order greater than two, concluding that "no meaningful classification" of compact C_p-equivariant H F_p-modules exists at odd primes (in contrast with the p=2 work of Dugger, Hazel, and May).
- quote: "in contrast to recent work at the prime 2 by Dugger, Hazel, and the second author, no meaningful classification of compact C_p-equivariant H F_p-modules exists at odd primes"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17604
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-22 (v1); 2026-05-08 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [9] For separable von Neumann algebras, Sasyk and Tørnquist (2009, Bull. Symbolic Logic 15(2):169-183) attack the isomorphism problem with Borel reducibility and Hjorth's turbulence theory — the standard descriptive-set-theoretic machinery for proving that no classification by countable invariants is possible.
- quote: "Our results are obtained by applying the notion of Borel reducibility and Hjorth's theory of turbulence to the isomorphism relation for separable von Neumann algebras."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.2657
- source_type: primary
- published: 2009-09-14 (journal ref: BSL 15(2), June 2009)
- confidence: medium

### [10] The positive counter-model where a CFSG-style program worked: Thurston's geometrization (conjectured 1982, proved by Perelman 2003) decomposes every oriented prime closed 3-manifold along 2-tori into pieces carrying one of exactly 8 Thurston geometries — a finite "periodic table" of building blocks analogous in spirit to the CFSG.
- quote: "Every oriented prime closed 3-manifold can be cut along 2-tori, so that the interior of each of the resulting manifolds has a geometric structure with finite volume. There are 8 possible geometric structures in 3 dimensions."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrization_conjecture
- source_type: secondary
- published: Thurston 1982; Perelman 2003; page rev. 2025-12-29
- confidence: high

### [11] The geometrization success is dimension-specific: in four dimensions only a rather restricted class of closed 4-manifolds admit a geometric decomposition, and Filipkiewicz (1983) found 18 plus one countably infinite family of 4-dimensional maximal model geometries (versus exactly 8 in dimension 3) — the finite-list paradigm breaks.
- quote: "In four dimensions, only a rather restricted class of closed 4-manifolds admit a geometric decomposition. However, lists of maximal model geometries can still be given. The four-dimensional maximal model geometries were classified by Richard Filipkiewicz in 1983. They number eighteen, plus one countably infinite family"
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrization_conjecture
- source_type: secondary
- published: Filipkiewicz 1983 (Warwick PhD thesis); Hillman arXiv:math/0212142
- confidence: high

### [12] Knots show the "decidable but unclassified" middle regime: unknot recognition is decidable (Haken 1961, normal surfaces), in NP (Hass–Lagarias–Pippenger 1999, JACM 46(2):185-211) and in coNP (Kuperberg 2014 under GRH; Lackenby 2021, Adv. Math. 387:107796, unconditional), but membership in P remains open, and Lackenby's announced (2021) quasi-polynomial algorithm is still unpublished as of October 2025.
- quote: "Hass, Lagarias & Pippenger (1999) showed that the unknotting problem is in the complexity class NP. ... A major unresolved challenge is to determine if the problem admits a polynomial time algorithm; that is, whether the problem lies in the complexity class P."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unknotting_problem
- source_type: secondary
- published: 1961/1999/2014/2021/2021; page rev. 2025-10-29
- confidence: high

- Knot/3-manifold census methodology (Hoste–Thistlethwaite tables, Burton census) as the model of "classification by enumeration plus invariants" — the realistic alternative to a structure theorem when wildness/undecidability does not apply but no periodic table exists.
