# F4: constructive QFT existence claims 2024–2026

## Findings

### [1] Clay's official Yang–Mills problem page still lists the problem as "Unsolved" as of 2026-10-29, and states no proof of the mass gap is known — i.e. even after the OpenAI announcement, no claim has been accepted by the prize body.
- quote: "Unsolved ... Experiment and computer simulations suggest the existence of a 'mass gap' ... But no proof of this property is known."
- url: https://www.claymath.org/millennium/yang-mills-the-maths-gap/
- source_type: primary
- published: page fetched 2026-10-29 (site copyright 2026)
- confidence: high

### [2] On 2026-10-26 OpenAI announced an AI-generated rigorous existence proof for four-dimensional interacting gauge theories with fermions — QCD, a simplest grand-unified completion of the Standard Model, and (as a subproblem) pure Yang–Mills — explicitly building on Balaban's constructive-QFT approach; the announcement claims existence only and makes no mass-gap claim.
- quote: "a mathematically rigorous existence proof of certain interacting quantum field theories containing gauge fields and fermions in four dimensions, including Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) and a unified completion of the Standard Model ... Our work builds on Balaban's approach in constructive quantum field theory. As a subproblem, this work also includes a proof of the existence of pure Yang–Mills theory"
- url: https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10-26
- confidence: high

### [3] The OpenAI Yang–Mills claim's exact rung as of 2026-10-29: press/company announcement + 1147-page unpublished manuscript + a claimed completed Lean formalization (finished 2026-10-16) that is NOT publicly released; no journal, no public machine-checkable artifact, no independent verification. Timeline claimed: natural-language YM-existence proof 2026-09-09, QCD proof 2026-09-18, Lean formalization of YM existence 2026-10-16.
- quote: "Our models produced a natural language proof for Yang–Mills existence on September 9th, 2026, and then subsequently produced a proof for QCD on September 18th, 2026 ... We finished our formalization of Yang–Mills' existence on October 16th, 2026" / "For the physics collaboration, the group has decided to hold off on releasing the AI-produced artifacts"
- url: https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10-26
- confidence: high

### [4] The KITP-shepherding statement (Gross chair; Douglas, Jaffe, Klebanov, Nekrasov; Oct 2026) confirms the existence proof incl. fermion coupling, says Balaban's 1980s RG ideas were "among the important elements" of it, and states flatly that confinement and the mass gap remain open; the proof and its Lean formalization will only be released after the committee is "convinced that it is correct", so verification is pending, not done.
- quote: "Balaban's ideas were among the important elements that recently enabled OpenAI to follow the theory all the way to infinite distances and prove the existence of its proper continuum limit. But confinement and the mass gap remain very important open challenges." / "Before we release it, we would like to be convinced that it is correct."
- url: https://www.kitp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/YM_working_group.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10 (statement); PDF hosted 2026-10-28
- confidence: high

### [5] Scientific American (2026-10-27) confirms the Clay problem has two required parts and OpenAI claimed only existence — "the part of the Clay problem that remains open" is the mass gap — and reports AGMAI (OpenAI's own advisory group) demanding immediate release of the withheld paper ("there is no scientific reason for delaying ... any further").
- quote: "This question comes down to two parts, referred to as 'existence' and 'mass gap.' The Clay Mathematics Institute's definition of the problem asks for answers to both. Today OpenAI claimed to prove the first of the two ... The part of the Clay problem that remains open is more quantitative"
- url: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/dueling-proofs-from-openai-and-anthropic-astound-scientists/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-10-27
- confidence: high

### [6] Per Scientific American, the closest independent competitor was an LLM-assisted effort to complete "Balaban's program" — Ethan Sussman (Northwestern) with Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) via a public GitHub repository — which believed it was near completion before OpenAI's unpublished claim "beat them to the finish line"; Roland Bauerschmidt (NYU) on Balaban's program: "He basically did it. But I think there's no one who understands these papers and can tell whether they're correct or not."
- quote: "Recently, sensing that a race was on to finish Balaban's program, some experts began using LLMs to churn out the necessary tomes of tedious mathematical steps ... They were making considerable progress and believed they were near to finishing the program. OpenAI's claim—with no publicly available proof—appears to have beaten them to the finish line."
- url: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/dueling-proofs-from-openai-and-anthropic-astound-scientists/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-10-27
- confidence: high

### [7] Douglas's peer-reviewed pre-claim survey (Nat. Rev. Phys. 8, 86–97, published 2026-01-12) establishes the state of the art before OpenAI: rigorous renormalization "has never been made rigorous in the needed generality" and on the mass gap "it is widely felt that no clear path is known towards proving it"; most-cited recent rigorous progress is in probability theory (stochastic quantization, rigorous strong coupling expansion, Chatterjee-style lattice/large-N programs), not continuum 4D existence.
- quote: "This requires renormalization, which has never been made rigorous in the needed generality." / "The mass gap property ... is widely felt that no clear path is known towards proving it."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-025-00909-2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-12
- confidence: high

### [8] Faizal & Shabir (arXiv:2606.19362, 2026-06-09; journal-ref Fortsch. Phys. 74 (2026) 4, e70097, with four companion papers in Int. J. Geom. Meth. Mod. Phys. 2650111–2650114) claim a full reflection-positive construction of 4D SU(N) Yang–Mills with BOTH mass gap and confinement, plus Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction — rung: journal-published claim (5 papers), no independent verification, not treated by the community or Clay as solving the Millennium problem; this is a mass-gap-claiming rival to the OpenAI existence-only claim, predating it by ~4 months.
- quote: "starting from a reflection-positive lattice formulation of pure SU(N) Yang-Mills theory we obtain a transfer operator with a uniform gap ... the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction turns these Euclidean facts into a Minkowski theory ... the spectral gap lying above the vacuum"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19362
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-09
- confidence: high (that the claim exists and is journal-refereed) / low (that the proof is correct)

### [9] Jacobsen, arXiv:2506.00284 (2025-05-30) claimed a constructive proof of existence AND mass gap for pure SU(3) on R^4 via a 5D orbifold regulator + polymer expansion + OS reconstruction — but the preprint was WITHDRAWN by arXiv admins "because it does not meet arXiv's research content quality standards"; rung: withdrawn preprint, claims mass gap, zero acceptance.
- quote: "arXiv admin note: This submission has been withdrawn because it does not meet arXiv's research content quality standards"

### [10] Nissim, arXiv:2510.22788 (2025-10-26) proves a mass gap, a unique infinite-volume limit, and a new large-N limit for U(N) LATTICE Yang–Mills in the 't Hooft regime (extending Cao–Sheffield–Zhu-style Langevin-mixing methods) — rigorous and real, but lattice + 't Hooft regime only; it does NOT touch the continuum 4D existence or continuum mass gap of the Clay problem.
- quote: "We establish a mass gap, prove the existence of a unique infinite volume limit, and give a new proof of the large N limit for U(N) lattice Yang-Mills theory in the 't Hooft regime."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22788
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-10-26
- confidence: high

### [11] Cao, Nissim & Sheffield, arXiv:2509.04688 (2025-09-04, v2 2025-09-28, marked "Submitted") extend the [SZZ23] dynamical approach to prove Wilson's area law in the 't Hooft regime for U(N), SU(N), SO(2N) by verifying the Dobrushin–Federbush (DF80) lattice mass-gap condition — again a lattice/regime-restricted theorem (confinement input), not a continuum mass gap.
- quote: "the dynamical approach to lattice Yang-Mills set forth in [SZZ23] may also be applied to prove Wilson's area law in the 't Hooft regime of parameters. The main point is to verify the mass gap condition from [DF80]"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04688
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-04
- confidence: high

### [12] Faria da Veiga & O'Carroll, arXiv:2509.03513 (2025-09-03, v2 2026-01-24), give partial 4D lattice SU(N) results toward the mass gap problem (charge-conjugation sector decomposition, Elitzur-type theorem, absence of local mass terms in the gluon-field expansion) and predict a multiplicity-two glueball state — explicitly partial ("Towards solving the mass gap problem"), no gap proved.
- quote: "Towards solving the mass gap problem, in the gluon field expansion of the action, we show there are no local terms, such as a mass term."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03513
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-03
- confidence: high

### [13] Nair, arXiv:2608.10133 (2026-08-10, v2 2026-08-22), gives "outlines of a proof" for a mass gap in 3D Yang–Mills via an eigenvalue inequality on the Laplacian of gauge-invariant configuration space — rung: 11-page preprint outline, claims mass gap in 3d only, not the 4D Clay problem.
- quote: "We give the outlines of a proof for the existence of a mass gap for the 3d Yang-Mills theory in terms of an inequality on the eigenvalues of the Laplacian on the gauge-invariant configuration space."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10133
- source_type: primary
