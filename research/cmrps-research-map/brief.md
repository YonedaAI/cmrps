# Research brief — CMRPS structural classification map

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Depth mode:** deep (7 angles, up to 8 queries each, 25+ sources target)

## Refined question

The Classification of Mathematical Representations of Physical Systems (CMRPS) program asks
whether mathematical representations of physical systems admit a rigorous structural
classification analogous to the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (CFSG): fundamental
families, irreducible building blocks, admissible constructions, and equivalences/dualities.
What is already classified in mathematics and mathematical physics, what is claimed, what is
proved, and where are the genuine obstructions?

## Scope

**In:** established classification theorems and their invariants (finite groups, Lie theory,
operator algebras, bundles, symplectic/Poisson geometry, tensor categories); reconstruction
theorems; category-/topos-/groupoid-based approaches to physics; AQFT and constructive QFT
frameworks; the precise status of 2024–2026 constructive-QFT existence claims (Balaban
program, Yang–Mills, QCD, GUT completions); information-geometric and entanglement-based
spacetime reconstruction; non-classifiability/undecidability obstructions.

**Out:** speculative theory-of-everything claims without mathematical content; pure
phenomenology; engineering applications; anything not bearing on classification structure.

## Assumptions

- Audience: research mathematicians writing a 12-volume series (YonedaAI Collaboration, PI
  Matthew Long).
- "Classification" means: families + invariants + construction operations + completeness
  statements, as in CFSG — not mere taxonomy.
- Claim-status discipline: announced ≠ manuscript ≠ machine-checked ≠ independently verified ≠
  accepted. Every claim of existence must be placed on that ladder.
- The YonedaAI internal programs (Yoneda Constraint, Emergent Spacetime, etc.) are handled from
  local context files, not this web research.

## Angles

- **F1** — CFSG as the model classification program: its architecture (families + sporadics + constructions), what invariants classify finite simple groups, and how Lie groups / representation theory / symplectic and Poisson geometry classification theorems are structured (objects, morphisms, invariants, completeness).
- **F2** — Quantum-side classification theorems: finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and quantum channels, C*-algebras (Gelfand–Naimark), von Neumann factor classification, tensor categories and superselection sectors — what is fully classified, what is partial, what is open.
- **F3** — Gauge and QFT mathematical structures: classification of principal bundles and connections (characteristic classes), anomalies, and the algebraic QFT (Haag–Kastler) framework's structural results (duality, superselection, the Doplicher–Roberts reconstruction).
- **F4** — Status of recent constructive quantum field theory existence claims (2024–2026): Balaban-program methods, announced Yang–Mills existence/mass-gap proofs, QCD and GUT completions, Clay Millennium problem status — announced vs manuscript vs machine-checked vs accepted, exactly.
- **F5** — Category-theoretic and reconstruction approaches: Yoneda embedding uses in physics, functorial QFT (Atiyah axioms, cobordism hypothesis), topos/groupoid quantum mechanics (Isham–Döring, Butterfield), and classical reconstruction theorems (Gelfand–Naimark, Stone, reconstruction from observables).
- **F6** — Spacetime and information geometry: what is proved vs conjectural in entanglement/tensor-network/holographic spacetime reconstruction; classification of Lorentzian/causal/metric structures; information-geometry correspondences.
- **F7** — Obstructions to classification: undecidability and wildness results in algebra (wild representation problems, undecidable group properties, non-classifiability theorems), and lessons from other multi-family classification programs (3-manifolds, knots) for when a CFSG-style theorem is possible.
