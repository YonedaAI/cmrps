# The Periodic Table of Mathematical Physics
### A Classification Program for Principia Physica
**YonedaAI Research Collective**

The finite simple group classification in your image provides an excellent organizational model for our research program.

The objective would be to construct an analogous **periodic table of mathematical structures underlying physical systems**, organized by fundamental families, their relationships, and their capacity to generate more complex theories.

The crucial difference is that finite simple groups have a proven exhaustive classification. Our proposed physical classification would initially be a research framework, with completeness as a theorem to investigate.

## 1. Proposed classification

We can organize the program into six foundational research families.

| Family | Research program | Mathematical foundations | Classification objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Yoneda Constraint | Category theory, representability, relational structure | Characterize physical systems through their admissible relationships |
| **II** | Emergent Spacetime | Information geometry, quantum entanglement, reconstruction | Identify conditions under which spacetime geometry emerges |
| **III** | Gravity–Information–Geometry | Differential geometry, curvature, information theory | Characterize relationships between informational structures and gravitational dynamics |
| **IV** | Condensed Representation Theory | Sheaves, representations, topology, condensed mathematics | Classify representations and emergent physical observables |
| **V** | Information Geometry Correspondence | Observation functors, equivalence classes, information geometry | Classify empirically distinguishable physical systems |
| **VI** | Principia Physica | Higher category theory, geometry, quantum theory | Investigate a unified mathematical foundation for physical theories |

These are research programs rather than mutually exclusive mathematical families. A rigorous classification would need to distinguish the mathematical objects being classified from the theories used to study them.

## 3. An important refinement

Your finite-simple-group example suggests three organizational levels.

**Infinite structural families** would include mathematical frameworks such as symplectic geometry, operator algebras, gauge structures, and categories of representations.

**Exceptional structures** would identify mathematically unusual cases that do not fit the general families. We should not assume that physical theories possess a finite collection of exceptional objects analogous to the 26 sporadic groups.

**Universal constructions** would describe how mathematical structures combine, transform, and generate representations of more complex physical systems.

The resulting classification should preserve established mathematical distinctions while making relationships between frameworks explicit.

## 4. The central conjecture

A possible long-term objective is:

### Universal Structural Classification Conjecture

Every admissible mathematical representation of a physical system belongs, up to a specified notion of equivalence, to a class generated by an explicitly characterized collection of fundamental structural families and admissible constructions.

Symbolically,

\[
\boxed{
\forall P\in\mathbf{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}},
\quad
\exists \{F_i\},\mathcal C:
P\simeq\mathcal C(F_1,\ldots,F_n)
}
\]

The research must establish what counts as an admissible physical system, which constructions are allowed, and whether finite generation is possible.

## 5. How this connects to our existing research

The **Yoneda Constraint** could provide the relational characterization of mathematical objects.

**Condensed Representation Theory** could investigate how those objects are represented across geometric, topological, and analytic settings.

**Information Geometry Correspondence** could provide a framework for distinguishing mathematical equivalence from observational equivalence.

**Emergent Spacetime** and **Gravity–Information–Geometry** could investigate whether geometric and gravitational structures can be reconstructed from more primitive mathematical or informational relationships.

Finally, **Principia Physica** would investigate whether these results can be assembled into a coherent foundational theory.

That gives the research program a logical progression:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Relational Structure}
\rightarrow
\text{Representation}
\rightarrow
\text{Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Geometric Reconstruction}
\rightarrow
\text{Physical Dynamics}
}
\]

This sequence is a proposed research architecture, not yet a demonstrated derivation.
