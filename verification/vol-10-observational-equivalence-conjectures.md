# Conjectures — vol-10-observational-equivalence

Unproved statements of the volume, as text. None is used as a lemma in
lean/Cmrps/Vol10ObservationalEquivalence.lean; where the module needs their content, the
content is recorded as an explicit hypothesis (`PlainTensorCounterexample`) or as a
statement predicate (`chentsov_fisher_uniqueness`, `petz_sudar_parametricity`).

## C-1 — Isocategorical groups (Theorem 3.1, Etingof–Gelaki)

There exist finite groups `G` and `H` that are not isomorphic and whose complex
representation categories are equivalent as plain tensor categories (monoidal categories,
ignoring the commutativity constraint). Literature proof: Etingof–Gelaki, triangular Hopf
algebra construction (arXiv:math/0007196); the paper uses it by reference and does not
reproduce the construction. Formalizing it would instantiate the Lean structure
`Cmrps.Vol10.PlainTensorCounterexample` and thereby discharge the hypotheses of
`eg_plain_tensor_insufficient` and `preserved_layer_discipline`.

## C-2 — Chentsov uniqueness (Theorem 5.1)

Up to a positive constant multiple, the Fisher information metric is the unique
Riemannian metric on spaces of probability distributions that is monotone under
stochastic maps. Literature proof: Chentsov's rigidity argument, used in the form stated
and applied at Petz–Sudar (arXiv:quant-ph/0102132). The Lean name
`Cmrps.Vol10.chentsov_fisher_uniqueness` records the statement as a predicate over
candidate quadratic forms; the monotonicity notion is external to the skeleton.

## C-3 — Petz–Sudar classification (Theorem 5.2)

Every quantum monotone metric arises from an operator monotone function `f` with
`f(1) = 1` and `f(x) = x f(x⁻¹)` through the Morozova–Chentsov form, and distinct such
`f` give distinct metrics on self-adjoint directions. The family is parametric, not a
finite list, and no member is distinguished among all monotone metrics. Literature proof:
Petz–Sudar (arXiv:quant-ph/0102132). The Lean name
`Cmrps.Vol10.petz_sudar_parametricity` records the statement as a predicate; the proof
ingredient that `f` and `f^#` give the same quadratic form on symmetric directions is
machine-checked as `Cmrps.Vol10.petz_transpose_same_form`, and one concrete instance of
metric distinctness is machine-checked as `Cmrps.Vol10.monotone_not_canonical`.

## C-4 — Doplicher–Roberts reconstruction (Remark 3.3)

With the symmetric structure restored, representation categories with conjugates, direct
sums, subobjects, and endomorphism ring of the tensor unit equal to the complex numbers
are equivalent to finite-dimensional unitary representations of a compact supergroup,
unique up to isomorphism. Literature proof: Doplicher–Roberts. The failure of Theorem 3.1
is specific to dropping the symmetric layer.

## C-5 — Non-transitivity of metric tolerance relations (§5.4)

On a connected state space, the tolerance relation `{(ρ,σ) : d_f(ρ,σ) ≤ ε}` is not
transitive for any `ε > 0`; hence by Proposition 4.2 it cannot be the relation of any
observation functor. The paper asserts the analysis; no formal proof is in the module.

## C-6 — Character tables do not separate D8 and Q8 (§9.3)

The dihedral and quaternion groups of order 8 realize the same character table (five
conjugacy classes of sizes 1, 1, 2, 2, 2; four linear characters; one degree-two
character with values (2, -2, 0, 0, 0)) and differ on element orders (five elements of
order 2 and two of order 4 in `D8`; one of order 2 and six of order 4 in `Q8`), hence are
non-isomorphic. Checked by the finite computation `invariant_coarseness.py`
(computational-evidence); a Lean proof would follow from order-of-elements lemmas for
Mathlib's `DihedralGroup` and `QuaternionGroup`. A shadow of C-1, not the theorem itself.

## C-7 — The correct choice of physical equivalence (§11)

Which candidate relation `≃` on system records is physical equivalence, i.e. where inside
the band of Definition 2.5 the quotient `Ob(Phys)/≃` should sit. The choice is open in
the series; no result of the volume fixes it.

## C-8 — Composition of indistinguishability under interaction (§4.3, §8.2)

Whether indistinguishability generated by admissible experiments composes in interacting
regimes. Proposition 4.6 gives the two regimes conditionally: safe under product records,
fail when a joint experiment not determined by factor records exists. Which regime holds
is a property of the interaction data; the series records the question as unstudied.

## C-9 — Positive reconstruction criterion (§11.1)

A positive criterion for "the same physical system" that survives the three-pass
screening protocol of §6.1. Success requires a criterion argued for at the empirical
layer; failure closes another row of the counterexample search negatively.

## C-10 — A system-level duality pair (§6, Table 4 row 1)

A pair of system records inside one formal framework, proved non-isomorphic and declared
physically equivalent — dual presentations at the system level. Gelfand duality supplies
the mathematical illustration (a compact Hausdorff space and its function algebra), but
the search did not produce a system-level pair; the row is only partially closed.
