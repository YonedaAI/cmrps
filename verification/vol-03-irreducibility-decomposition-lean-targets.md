At most 10 targets.

1. `finite_dim_cstar_block_decomposition` (paper: Theorem 3.3; Haskell: `blockDecomposition`)
   Every unital finite-dimensional complex C*-algebra A is *-isomorphic to a direct sum of
   matrix algebras `M_{n_i}(C)`, with sum of n_i^2 equal to dim A and number of blocks equal
   to dim of the center.

2. `dimension_vector_uniqueness` (paper: Theorem 3.3, uniqueness clause; Haskell: `dimVector`)
   The multiset of matrix block sizes of a unital finite-dimensional complex C*-algebra is
   unique up to permutation and is preserved by *-isomorphism.

3. `dimension_vector_complete_invariant` (paper: Corollary 3.4; Haskell: `dimensionVectorComplete`)
   Two unital finite-dimensional complex C*-algebras are *-isomorphic if and only if their
   dimension vectors agree as multisets.

4. `unital_star_hom_classification` (paper: Theorem 4.2; Haskell: `unitalStarHoms`, `MultiplicityMatrix`)
   Unital *-homomorphisms A -> B between decomposed finite-dimensional C*-algebras, up to
   unitary conjugacy in B, correspond bijectively to multiplicity matrices R = (r_i^j) of
   nonnegative integers with n_i r_i^j the rank contributions and column sums equal to the
   codomain block sizes m_j.

5. `outer_automorphism_permutation` (paper: Corollary 4.3; Haskell: `outerAutomorphisms`)
   Automorphisms of a decomposed algebra modulo inner automorphisms are classified by
   permutations of the blocks with equal size.

6. `tensor_block_product` (paper: Proposition 8.1; Haskell: `tensorBlock`, `directSumBlocks`)
   M_n(C) tensor M_m(C) is *-isomorphic to M_{nm}(C); dimension vectors of a direct sum are
   the multiset union and of a tensor product the pairwise product multiset.

7. `closure_semigroup` (paper: Corollary 8.2; Haskell: `closureSemigroup`)
   The closure of a finite set of matrix blocks under direct sum and tensor product consists
   exactly of the algebras whose dimension-vector entries lie in the multiplicative semigroup
   generated by the block sizes.

8. `states_direct_sum_join` (paper: Proposition 3.7; Haskell: `statesDirectSum`)
   Every state of a direct sum A_1 + A_2 is a convex combination of normalized block states
   with weights the expectations of the central block projections; the weights and normalized
   restrictions are unique.

9. `module_factorization` (paper: Proposition 4.5; Haskell: `moduleFactorization`)
   Every finite-dimensional module over a decomposed algebra is the direct sum of its central
   block summands, each a multiple of the unique simple module of its block, with unique
   multiplicities.

10. `kraus_normal_form_uniqueness` (paper: Theorem 6.2; Haskell: `krausNormalForm`)
    Every completely positive map between matrix algebras has a Kraus representation whose
    minimal length equals the rank of the Choi matrix, and two minimal Kraus families of the
    same map are related by a unitary change of frame. (The negative companion, that Kraus
    rank is not an isomorphism classification of channels, is a prohibition and not a
    mechanization target.)
