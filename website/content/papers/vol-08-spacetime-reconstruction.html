Abstract

This paper classifies and reconstructs spacetime and separates what is proved from what is proposed. Four results are complete within their stated hypotheses: connected holonomy groups of Lorentzian manifolds in signature \((1,n-1)\) are classified and realized by explicit metrics; the causal ladder of spacetimes is complete as a hierarchy of causality conditions; closed 3-manifolds decompose into exactly eight Thurston geometries; and for two, three, and four boundary regions the holographic entropy cone is cut out by strong subadditivity together with monogamy of mutual information. Against these islands, the homeomorphism problem for closed 4-manifolds is algorithmically undecidable, so finite classification tables exist only in tame regimes. Reconstruction recovers a target geometry from relational probe data: the chronological order determines the topology and the conformal class of a distinguishing spacetime, and entanglement-wedge reconstruction recovers bulk operators from boundary subregions in a quantum-information setting. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula remains a proposal, with exact agreement in AdS\(_3\)/2D CFT and for spherical regions in higher dimensions, HaPPY codes realize it in toy models only, and entanglement-induced geometry is an argument. Two boundary statements delimit the subject: monogamy of mutual information is independent of strong subadditivity over entropic vectors, and the ladder depends on how its rungs are translated to discrete orders.

Introduction

Lorentzian geometry admits no uniform classification by local invariants: the causal, metric, and holonomic structures of a spacetime constrain its metric only up to large equivalence classes. Riemannian manifolds and Lie algebras carry discrete or rigid classification invariants. Lorentzian spacetimes offer global invariants instead: holonomy groups, causal ladder rungs, Thurston model geometries. Each of these determines geometry only within a restricted class or up to conformal rescaling. The questions are which of these invariants classify their stated targets, which of them reconstruct a geometry from relational data, and where the classification runs out.

The evidence divides cleanly. On one side stand four complete results inside their hypotheses. Connected holonomy groups of Lorentzian manifolds are classified and every entry is realized by a metric [1]. The causal ladder of spacetimes is complete: each rung strengthens the previous one and strict counterexamples separate the rungs [2]. Closed 3-manifolds decompose into exactly eight model geometries [3], [4]. For at most four boundary regions the holographic entropy cone is cut out by two named inequalities [6]. On the other side stands a sharp failure: the homeomorphism problem for closed 4-manifolds is algorithmically undecidable, and Tancer’s construction needs as few as nine summands of \(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2\) [5]. That bound limits the topological complexity an undecidability proof requires; it does not separate decidable from undecidable manifolds, and the recognition problem for \(\mathbb{S}^4\) itself remains open. Finite tables of model geometries therefore exist in dimension three and not in dimension four.

Metric reconstruction from non-metric data succeeds classically up to a conformal factor and remains partially conjectural in the quantum setting. In distinguishing spacetimes of dimension \(n \ge 3\) the chronological order determines the manifold topology and the conformal metric class [8], [9]. Holographic reconstruction operates rigorously only inside restricted code subspaces: boundary subregions reconstruct bulk operators in their entanglement wedge [11], while the identification of bulk areas with boundary entropies remains an unproved variational prescription [10], its toy-model realizations [12] and its entanglement arguments [13] notwithstanding. The proved determination theorems and the proposals are kept in separate sections below, and the proposals never carry the theorems’ authority.

This paper classifies mathematical representations of physical systems rather than physical systems themselves. A system is recorded as a tuple \(P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})\) of state space, observables, dynamics, symmetries, and composition rules; spacetime systems carry manifolds, metrics, causal relations, and holonomy groups in the state-space slot. Theorems cited from the literature appear with their hypotheses and their scope. Proposals and arguments are labeled as such in every section where they appear. Statements proved here carry complete proofs, and standard counterexamples are attributed to their sources.

The relation to prior work is one of scope. The classification theorems used here are complete results about narrow targets: holonomy groups [1], causality conditions [2], model geometries [3], [4], and entropy cones [6]. The reconstruction theorems determine geometry from relational data [8] and represent bulk operators on boundary regions [11]. The holographic literature adds a prescription [10], solvable models [12], and a research argument [13]. What no source supplies is a classification of Lorentzian spacetimes up to isometry, and what this paper adds is a set of boundary results: invariants that fail to be complete (11), inequalities that fail to be redundant (29), and a ladder that fails to transfer to discrete models (22). Boundaries of this kind are what a classification program needs before it can state honest targets.

Two boundary statements are made precise here. Monogamy of mutual information is not implied by strong subadditivity even over classical entropic vectors; the perfectly correlated triple of 29 is the standard counterexample of the holographic-entropy literature [7], [6], recorded with a short self-contained derivation. The causal ladder, read as a hierarchy of order-theoretic conditions, depends on how its rungs are translated to discrete orders: the compactness rung holds universally in finite orders while the strict-chronology reading of distinguishing fails there (20, 21).

The paper also separates three notions that are routinely conflated in emergent-spacetime discussions: classification of geometries by invariants, reconstruction of geometry from probe data, and derivation of a theory’s dynamics from information. The first two have proved instances in this volume and the third does not. Executable checks of the entropy inequalities, finite-order causal checks, and a typed taxonomy of the volume’s systems accompany the arguments as computational evidence.

Definitions

The definitions state each notion explicitly; classical notions follow standard usage.

Definition 1 (Lorentzian spacetime). A Lorentzian spacetime is a connected, time-orientable smooth manifold \(M\) of dimension \(n \ge 2\) equipped with a Lorentzian metric \(g\) of signature \((1, n-1)\). The points of \(M\) are events. Two spacetimes \((M,g)\) and \((M',g')\) are conformally equivalent when a diffeomorphism pulls one metric back to a positive conformal multiple \(e^{2f} g'\) of the other. They are isometric when the conformal factor is constant equal to one.

Definition 2 (Causal relations). For events \(p, q \in M\) write \(p \prec q\) when a future-directed timelike curve joins \(p\) to \(q\), and \(p \le q\) when a future-directed causal curve joins them. Write \(I^+(p) = \{ q : p \prec q \}\) and \(J^+(p) = \{ q : p \le q \}\), and symmetrically for the pasts. The relations \(\prec\) and \(\le\) are the causal order of the spacetime.

Definition 3 (The causal ladder). A Lorentzian spacetime is chronological when no closed timelike curve exists, causal when no closed causal curve exists, distinguishing when \(I^-(p) = I^-(q)\) implies \(p = q\) and \(I^+(p) = I^+(q)\) implies \(p = q\) (past-distinguishing and future-distinguishing), strongly causal when every event has a neighborhood base of causally convex open sets, stably causal when a time function exists, causally continuous when distinguishing and the maps \(p \mapsto I^{\pm}(p)\) are outer continuous (the spacetime is reflecting), causally simple when distinguishing and each \(J^+(p)\), \(J^-(p)\) is closed, and globally hyperbolic when the spacetime is causal and \(J^+(p) \cap J^-(q)\) is compact for all \(p, q\). These conditions form the causal ladder, ordered from chronology to global hyperbolicity [2]. Causality is part of the definition of global hyperbolicity: compact diamonds alone do not exclude closed causal curves.

Definition 4 (Holonomy). For a Lorentzian spacetime \((M,g)\) the holonomy group \(\mathrm{Hol}(g)\) is the subgroup of \(\mathrm{O}(1,n-1)\) obtained by parallel transport of the Levi-Civita connection along piecewise smooth loops at a base point; the restricted holonomy \(\mathrm{Hol}^0(g)\) is the identity component. The metric \(g\) is decomposable when the tangent bundle splits as an orthogonal direct sum of proper nondegenerate subspaces that are parallel for the connection, equivalently when \(\mathrm{Hol}(g)\) preserves such a splitting; a Lorentzian splitting consists of a Lorentzian factor and one or more Riemannian factors. Otherwise \(g\) is indecomposable.

Definition 5 (Entropy vectors). Let \(A_1, \dots, A_n\) be boundary regions and let \(S\) assign a nonnegative real number \(S(A)\) to every nonempty union \(A = A_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup A_{i_k}\), with \(S(\emptyset) = 0\). The tuple \(S \in \mathbb{R}^{2^n - 1}\) is an entropy vector. An entropy vector is entropic when it is realized by the von Neumann entropy of some quantum state on the tensor product of the region Hilbert spaces. Standard inequalities include subadditivity \(S(A) + S(B) \ge S(AB)\), the Araki-Lieb bound \(|S(A) - S(B)| \le S(AB)\), and strong subadditivity \[ S(U) + S(V) \ge S(U \cup V) + S(U \cap V), \] for all unions \(U, V\) of the \(A_i\). Mutual information is \(I(A{:}B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB)\), and conditional mutual information is \(I(A{:}B \mid C) = S(AC) + S(BC) - S(ABC) - S(C)\). Monogamy of mutual information for three disjoint regions \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) (single or composite unions of the \(A_i\)) is the inequality \[ S(A) + S(B) + S(C) + S(ABC) \le S(AB) + S(AC) + S(BC). \] The inequality applies to every choice of disjoint \(A\), \(B\), \(C\); for single regions it reads as stated, and for composite regions it gives the coarse-grained instances used at \(n = 4\).

Definition 6 (Holographic entropy cone). Fix \(n\) boundary regions of a conformal field theory with a smooth static classical Einstein dual. The Ryu-Takayanagi prescription assigns each region \(A\) the area of a minimal bulk surface homologous to \(A\) in a static slice; the resulting vectors \(S\) lie in the holographic entropy cone \(\mathcal{H}_n \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{2^n-1}\), the closure of vectors realized by such surfaces. An RT inequality is a linear inequality valid on \(\mathcal{H}_n\) [6]. Staticity keeps the minimal-area prescription of [10] in force; a covariant extremal-surface prescription exists [14] and is not used in this paper.

Definition 7 (Reconstruction from probes). A probe datum for a class of spacetimes is a rule assigning to each spacetime relational data \(D(M,g)\) that does not name the metric: a causal order, a family of curves, an algebra of observables, a set of entropies. A reconstruction of a target \(T\) from \(D\) is a procedure which recovers \(T\) from \(D(M,g)\) for every spacetime in a stated class, together with a proof that the recovery is faithful for a stated equivalence: isometry, conformal isometry, or isomorphism. A reconstruction theorem must state its class, its datum, and its equivalence; a reconstruction claim without all three is incomplete.

Definition 8 (Systems of this volume). The physical systems of this volume are tuples \(P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})\) in which \(X\) carries geometric data: a smooth manifold with a Lorentzian metric, a causal relation, a holonomy group, or one of the Thurston geometry types. Entropy-vector systems carry measure-theoretic data and are included because the holographic cone is defined on them. All equivalences used in this volume are stated in the results that use them; none is assumed silently.

Definition 9 (Spacetime classification problem). Fix a dimension \(n\) and an equivalence \(\simeq\) among isometry, conformal equivalence, and diffeomorphism. The classification problem \(\mathrm{Class}(\mathrm{Lor}_n / \simeq)\) is the problem of describing the equivalence classes of Lorentzian spacetimes of dimension \(n\) by a complete set of invariants: a list of values such that two spacetimes carry the same values exactly when they are \(\simeq\)-equivalent. A solution must state the equivalence, the invariant, and the class; a list of invariants that is constant on classes but not separating on them is a partial solution only.

Remark 10 (Claim status). The program distinguishes a proved theorem from a proposal and an argument. In this paper, theorems are cited with their hypotheses and their sources, propositions proved here carry complete proofs, and the conjectural holographic material is marked as proposal or argument in every section where it appears. Computational evidence is evidence and is never presented as proof.

Incomplete invariants

Invariants do not classify unless they are complete. The holonomy group, the causal class, and the dimension fail to be complete: an explicit pair of spacetimes shares all three and is not isometric.

Proposition 11 (Shared invariants, distinct spacetimes). There exist two Lorentzian spacetimes with the same dimension, the same holonomy group, and the same causal class, which are not isometric. Concretely, the flat plane \(\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\) with metric \(-dt^2 + dx^2\) and the flat cylinder \(\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{S}^1\) with metric \(-dt^2 + d\theta^2\) both have trivial holonomy and are both globally hyperbolic, and no isometry exists between them.

Proof. Both metrics are flat: the Riemann tensor vanishes in the coordinates displayed, so parallel transport is path-independent and the holonomy group of each is the identity. Both are globally hyperbolic: \(t\) is a time function on each space, every inextendible causal curve meets each level set of \(t\) exactly once, the level sets are Cauchy surfaces, and each causal diamond is closed with \(t\) bounded and with spatial coordinate in a compact circle or an interval, hence compact. The two spaces are not diffeomorphic: the fundamental group of the cylinder is \(\mathbb{Z}\) and the fundamental group of the plane is trivial, and an isometry would in particular be a diffeomorphism. Hence the listed invariants do not determine the isometry class. ◻

Corollary 12 (Classification by these invariants is not classification). No classification of Lorentzian spacetimes up to isometry can be read off the holonomy group, the causal class, and the dimension alone. Any complete classification must add global invariants, such as topology or global curvature data, and the list of 1 must be read as a list of attached data rather than as a complete set of coordinates.

Proof. 11 exhibits two non-isometric spacetimes with identical values of every listed invariant, so the map from spacetimes to invariant values is not injective on isometry classes. ◻

The holonomy classification of 13 and the ladder of 18 are sharp exactly because their targets are narrow: possible holonomy groups, and possible causality regimes. 12 states the complementary limit: a sharp invariant classification of a feature is not a classification of the object. A complete classification in this sense would need additional global invariants, such as topology or global curvature data, in addition to the rows of 1.

Lorentzian holonomy

Holonomy is the classical invariant of a metric connection. For Lorentzian metrics it is completely classified [1], and this section records the classification together with two proved structural properties of the invariant.

Theorem 13 (Lorentzian holonomy classification). Let \((M,g)\) be a connected, simply connected Lorentzian manifold of signature \((1,n-1)\) with \(g\) indecomposable. Then the connected holonomy group \(\mathrm{Hol}^0(g)\) is one of the following: the full group \(\mathrm{SO}(1,n-1)^{\uparrow}\); a special holonomy group whose algebra stabilizes a null line and embeds in the similarity algebra \(\mathfrak{sim}(n-2) = (\mathbb{R} \oplus \mathfrak{so}(n-2)) \ltimes \mathbb{R}^{n-2}\); or, when \(g\) is locally symmetric, the isotropy holonomy of a symmetric space. Every entry of the list is the holonomy group of an explicitly constructed Lorentzian metric [1].

Argument structure. The classification proceeds in three steps. First, the holonomy algebra embeds in \(\mathfrak{so}(1,n-1)\) and is restricted by Berger’s criterion in its Lorentzian form: an algebra acting irreducibly is all of \(\mathfrak{so}(1,n-1)\), while a reducible indecomposable algebra must stabilize a null line, since a subalgebra preserving a timelike direction would split the metric. Second, the null-line-stabilizing case is classified by prolongation: the algebra embeds in \(\mathfrak{sim}(n-2)\) and its screen projection in \(\mathfrak{so}(n-2)\) is a Riemannian holonomy algebra, so the candidate list reduces to the Riemannian Berger list with the extra similarity data. Third, realization constructs a Walker or plane-wave type metric for each admitted algebra and checks its holonomy by direct computation of the curvature and its parallel translates. The complete argument, including the full entry-by-entry table and the realizing metrics, is in the survey of Galaev [1], which supplies the complete proof.

Remark 14 (Invariant, not classifier). The holonomy group classifies holonomy groups, not spacetimes. Many non-isometric metrics share a holonomy type, and the classification of 13 is a classification of the invariant recorded in 1. It is the spacetime analogue of classifying the possible values of an invariant, which is a different achievement from classifying the objects on which the invariant is evaluated.

Proposition 15 (Holonomy of a product). Let \((M_1, g_1)\) and \((M_2, g_2)\) be connected pseudo-Riemannian manifolds. On the product \(M_1 \times M_2\) with the product metric \(g_1 \oplus g_2\), the restricted holonomy satisfies \[\mathrm{Hol}^0(g_1 \oplus g_2) = \mathrm{Hol}^0(g_1) \times \mathrm{Hol}^0(g_2).\]

Proof. The Levi-Civita connection of a product metric is the direct sum of the factor connections, so parallel transport preserves the splitting \(T(M_1 \times M_2) = TM_1 \oplus TM_2\) of tangent spaces into horizontal and vertical parts. By the Ambrose-Singer theorem the restricted holonomy algebra is generated by parallel translates of curvature endomorphisms. The curvature of the product splits as \(R = R_1 \oplus R_2\), each factor curvature acting only on its own summand, so the generated algebra is \(\mathfrak{hol}^0(g_1) \oplus \mathfrak{hol}^0(g_2)\). Exponentiating gives the group identity for restricted holonomy. No completeness assumption is used; the identity concerns the restricted group, which is generated by contractible loops. ◻

15 is the invariant-level statement of what it means for a metric to decompose. Indecomposability in 4 is exactly the failure of this splitting, and the holonomy classification of 13 is a classification of the indecomposable case.

Lemma 16 (Restricted holonomy). For every Lorentzian spacetime the restricted holonomy \(\mathrm{Hol}^0(g)\) is a connected Lie subgroup of \(\mathrm{SO}(1,n-1)^{\uparrow}\), and time-orientability places the full holonomy \(\mathrm{Hol}(g)\) in the orthochronous group \(\mathrm{O}(1,n-1)^{\uparrow}\). The quotient \(\mathrm{Hol}(g)/\mathrm{Hol}^0(g)\) is countable in its intrinsic topology, and the classification of 13 concerns the connected part.

Proof. \(\mathrm{Hol}^0(g)\) is generated by parallel transport along contractible loops, hence connected and contained in the identity component \(\mathrm{SO}(1,n-1)^{\uparrow}\) on every Lorentzian manifold, independently of orientability. Time-orientability is exactly the condition that parallel transport preserves time-orientation on all loops, so \(\mathrm{Hol}(g)\) lies in the orthochronous group. The quotient of a Lie group by its identity component is countable in the intrinsic topology of the quotient, though it need not be discrete in the subspace topology of \(\mathrm{O}(1,n-1)\). ◻

Remark 17 (Two standard examples). Two metrics illustrate the cases of 13. The flat metric on Minkowski space has trivial holonomy and is both decomposable and locally symmetric, so it sits outside the indecomposable list of the theorem: its trivial holonomy is an instance of 15 applied to flat factors. A plane-wave metric (a Walker metric with parallel null vector) is indecomposable with a special holonomy group obtained by the prolongation construction, and its holonomy is the proper null-line-stabilizing entry predicted by 13 and realized by the metric itself [1]. The special entries of the list are attained by such metrics rather than merely possible.

The causal ladder

Causality conditions order spacetimes by how badly causal curves misbehave. The ladder is complete as a classification of these conditions [2], and its rungs do not survive translation to discrete orders.

Theorem 18 (Completeness of the causal ladder). For Lorentzian spacetimes the causality conditions of 3 form a strict chain: global hyperbolicity implies causal simplicity, which implies causal continuity, which implies stable causality, which implies strong causality, which implies distinguishing, which implies causality, which implies chronology. Each implication is strict: for each pair of adjacent rungs there exists a spacetime satisfying the lower rung and violating the higher one. Global hyperbolicity is the strongest of the standard causality conditions, and it is equivalent to the compactness of causal diamonds \(J^+(p) \cap J^-(q)\) together with the absence of closed causal curves [2].

Argument structure. The implications are proved in the source in a single framework with minimal hypotheses; the strictness is proved by constructing, for each pair of adjacent rungs, a separating spacetime, and the equivalences at the top rung are proved by the compactness characterization of global hyperbolicity. The survey of Minguzzi and Sánchez also records the alternative definitions of each rung and the smoothability results needed to make the examples smooth. The contribution of this paper begins at 20, where the ladder is tested against a different category of causal models.

Remark 19 (Separating examples). The strictness of 18 is witnessed by standard examples catalogued in [2]: spacetimes with closed timelike curves fail chronology, quotients with closed causal curves but no closed timelike curve fail causality only, and each higher rung has a separating example in which the lower rung holds and the higher one fails. The existence of these examples is what makes the ladder a classification of causality conditions rather than a list of definitions: each name refers to a genuinely distinct regime.

3 doubles as the checklist used by the finite-order tests of 20 and 21: the compactness rung passes on every finite order and the distinguishing rung fails on the antichain, which is the first row where the discrete translation breaks.

The ladder is a theorem about the category of Lorentzian manifolds. Causal set models and other discrete approaches to spacetime replace the manifold by a partially ordered set. The next two propositions show that the ladder’s rungs do not transfer to that regime.

Proposition 20 (Finite orders satisfy the compactness rung). Let \((X, \le)\) be a finite partially ordered set, read as a causal model with \(x < y\) meaning that \(x\) precedes \(y\). Then no strictly increasing loop \(x_1 < x_2 < \cdots < x_k < x_1\) exists, and every order interval \([x,y] = \{ z : x \le z \le y \}\) is a finite set. Consequently the order-theoretic analogues of chronology and of compact causal diamonds hold for every finite order.

Proof. A strictly increasing loop would give \(x_1 < x_1\) by transitivity, contradicting irreflexivity of a partial order. Every order interval is a subset of the finite set \(X\), hence finite, and a finite set is compact in every topology. Both claims use only the order axioms and finiteness. ◻

Proposition 21 (Distinguishing fails in finite orders under the strict reading). Read the ladder rungs on an order by translating the open futures and pasts literally: \(I^+(x) = \{ y : x < y \}\) and \(I^-(x) = \{ y : y < x \}\). Under this translation there exists a finite order satisfying the conclusions of 20 and failing distinguishing: for the two-element antichain \(X = \{a, b\}\) with \(a\) and \(b\) incomparable, one has \(I^-(a) = I^-(b) = \emptyset\) and \(I^+(a) = I^+(b) = \emptyset\) for distinct points.

Proof. In an antichain no two distinct points are related, so every chronological future and past is empty. Distinct points therefore carry identical pasts and identical futures, while the order-theoretic compactness of intervals holds vacuously. This is exactly the failure mode 3 forbids at the distinguishing rung. ◻

Corollary 22 (Translation dependence of the ladder on finite orders). Rung membership of the causal ladder is not invariant under the translation from continuum definitions to orders. The strict reading of 21 fails on finite orders while the compactness rung holds universally there, and the reflexive reading built from causal futures \(J^{\pm}(x) = \{ y : y \le x \}\) is distinguishing on every antisymmetric order by construction. A discrete causality theory must therefore state which translation it uses, and the rungs cannot be carried over verbatim from the manifold category.

Proof. Combine 20 and 21 with 18. In the manifold category global hyperbolicity implies distinguishing. In the finite-order reading, the analogue of global hyperbolicity holds on the antichain and the analogue of distinguishing fails. The implication structure therefore differs between the two categories. ◻

The result does not say that discrete models of spacetime are unreasonable. It says that a classification invariant proved in the manifold category is not automatically an invariant of the discrete one, and any program that imports the ladder into causal set theory must prove new theorems rather than cite [2]. The finite orders studied here carry no additional structure. Causal-set formulations of discrete spacetime impose local finiteness and a natural measure in addition to the order and define their own discrete causality conditions; those formulations lie outside the scope of this volume.

Geometrization in low dimensions

Dimension three admits a finite table of model geometries. Dimension four does not admit an algorithmic classification. This contrast is the sharpest classification boundary in the volume.

Theorem 23 (Geometrization of closed 3-manifolds). Every closed 3-manifold decomposes along a canonical family of spheres and tori into pieces, each of which admits one of exactly eight Thurston model geometries: \(\mathbb{E}^3\), \(\mathbb{S}^3\), \(\mathbb{H}^3\), \(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{R}\), \(\mathbb{H}^2 \times \mathbb{R}\), \(\mathrm{Nil}\), \(\mathrm{Sol}\), and \(\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2, \mathbb{R})\). The decomposition is unique up to isotopy, and each geometry is realized by a homogeneous metric on its model space [3], [4].

Argument structure. Thurston reduced the classification of closed 3-manifolds to the geometrization statement, proved by Perelman through Ricci flow with surgery: the flow breaks the manifold at controlled singularities, and the surgery pieces are precisely the pieces carrying one of the eight model geometries. The list of eight is exhaustive because a model geometry is a maximal simply connected homogeneous geometry, and the classification of such geometries in dimension three gives eight [4], [3].

Proposition 24 (Homogeneity of the model geometries). Each of the eight geometries of 4 is homogeneous: its isometry group acts transitively on the model space. The maximal isometry dimension in dimension three is six, attained by exactly the constant-curvature geometries \(\mathbb{E}^3\), \(\mathbb{S}^3\), and \(\mathbb{H}^3\).

Proof. Each model space is a simply connected Lie group or a symmetric space carrying an invariant metric of the stated signature (Riemannian here), and the isometry group of an invariant metric on a Lie group contains the left translations, which act transitively. The dimension counts in 4 are the dimensions of the full isometry groups of the model metrics, computed from the Killing fields of the corresponding homogeneous structures. In dimension three the isotropy of a homogeneous geometry is a subgroup of \(\mathrm{O}(3)\) of dimension at most three, so the isometry dimension is at most six, and the three constant-curvature geometries attain the bound because their isotropy is all of \(\mathrm{O}(3)\). The eight entries are precisely the maximal such geometries; maximality is the classification input of 23 [4]. ◻

Theorem 25 (Unrecognizability in dimension four). The recognition problem for closed 4-manifolds is algorithmically undecidable: there is no algorithm that takes as input a finite triangulation of a closed 4-manifold and decides whether it is homeomorphic to \(\#_9(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2)\). The connected sum \(\#_9(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2)\) is therefore unrecognizable, improving the previous bound \(\#_{12}(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2)\) of Gordon [5].

Argument structure. The construction encodes the Adian-Rabin set of a group presentation into the topology of a simply connected 4-manifold built from the presentation, and shows that the homeomorphism type of the resulting connected sum of copies of \(\mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2\) decides a property of the presentation that is known to be undecidable. Removing Markov’s trick and reducing the deficiency of the presentation lowers the number of summands from twelve to nine [5].

Corollary 26 (Dimension contrast). A finite table of model geometries with a canonical decomposition theorem exists for closed 3-manifolds and cannot exist, in the algorithmic sense, for closed 4-manifolds. Classification of closed manifolds by a finite table of models is a tame-regime phenomenon.

Proof. 23 supplies the finite table and the decomposition in dimension three. 25 shows that no algorithm can recognize the members of even a single infinite family of closed 4-manifolds, so no finite table can be accompanied by an algorithmic identification procedure in dimension four. ◻

Remark 27 (Model geometries in dimension four). Four-dimensional model geometries do exist as a list: Filipkiewicz classified them into eighteen geometries together with one countably infinite family [15]. The existence of a list of homogeneous models is not a classification of manifolds, and 25 shows that recognition of manifolds carrying those geometries is undecidable in general. Restricted classes do decompose; the unrestricted classification does not exist.

The holographic entropy cone

The holographic entropy cone is the one classification result on the quantum-information side of this volume whose completeness is proved in a bounded range. The bound matters: for two, three, and four regions the inequality set is known and small; five regions are settled by a later enumeration; from six regions on the facet lists are open. The explosion of facets from five regions on tracks the proliferation of minimal-graph topologies that minimal surfaces can realize [6].

Theorem 28 (Entropy cone for at most four regions). Fix \(n \in \{2, 3, 4\}\) boundary regions of a conformal field theory with a smooth static classical Einstein dual. The holographic entropy cone \(\mathcal{H}_n\) coincides with the polyhedral cone cut out by the RT inequalities, and these are exactly the strong subadditivity inequalities (1) together with the monogamy-of-mutual-information inequalities (2) in all their instances, including composite regions at \(n = 4\). Monogamy is a three-region inequality: it is already facet-defining at \(n = 3\) and is vacuous only at \(n = 2\). For every fixed \(n\) the number of independent RT inequalities is finite [6].

Argument structure. The proof reduces classical Einstein geometries to minimal graph models encoding the cutting and gluing relations of minimal surfaces, enumerates the extreme rays of the resulting cone for \(n \le 4\), and identifies the facet inequalities with strong subadditivity and monogamy of mutual information. The finiteness for fixed \(n\) follows from the finite combinatorial types of the graph models [6].

28 is a completeness statement, and completeness statements invite over-reading. The cone \(\mathcal{H}_n\) is defined through the Ryu-Takayanagi prescription, which is a proposal (10). What is proved is the combinatorial geometry of the set of vectors the prescription can produce in the classical limit. The next proposition shows that the second inequality family of 28 is genuinely additional.

Proposition 29 (Monogamy is independent of strong subadditivity). The monogamy-of-mutual-information inequality (2) for three single regions is not implied by strong subadditivity (1) over entropic vectors. An explicit witness is the entropy vector of a perfectly correlated triple, the standard counterexample of the holographic-entropy literature [7], [6]: let \(X\) be a uniform bit and set \(A = B = C = X\). Then \[S(A) = S(B) = S(C) = S(AB) = S(AC) = S(BC) = S(ABC) = 1,\] all strong subadditivity inequalities (1) hold, and (2) is violated: the left side equals \(4\) and the right side equals \(3\).

Proof. The vector is entropic: it is realized by the classical state of the random variable \(X\), so every entropy is the Shannon entropy of the indicated marginal, and each listed value equals \(1\). Strong subadditivity takes the form \(S(U) + S(V) \ge S(U \cup V) + S(U \cap V)\) for unions \(U, V\) of \(\{A,B,C\}\). If \(U \cap V = \emptyset\) then the right side is \(S(U \cup V) \le 1\) and the left side is \(2\). If \(U \cap V \ne \emptyset\) then both sides equal \(2\). So (1) holds in every case. Monogamy of mutual information reads \(S(A) + S(B) + S(C) + S(ABC) \le S(AB) + S(AC) + S(BC)\), that is \(4 \le 3\) on this vector, which is false. An entropic vector satisfying strong subadditivity and violating monogamy proves independence. ◻

Corollary 30 (Classical correlations violate monogamy). The witness of 29 is a classical random variable, so monogamy of mutual information is not valid for general quantum states, classical or otherwise. Its validity inside \(\mathcal{H}_n\) is a property of minimal surfaces in classical Einstein gravity, not a property of entropy theory. The completeness of 28 needs the monogamy family in addition to strong subadditivity already at \(n = 3\), because strong subadditivity alone leaves room for the witness.

Proof. The witness is classical by construction. Substituting it into (2) violates the inequality, so the inequality is not universally valid. 28 therefore cannot be stated with strong subadditivity alone. ◻

Proposition 31 (Two forms of monogamy). For three single regions, (2) is equivalent to the information form \[I(A{:}B) + I(A{:}C) \le I(A{:}BC),\] and both are equivalent to the negativity statement that the tripartite information \(I_3(A{:}B{:}C) = I(A{:}B) + I(A{:}C) - I(A{:}BC)\) is nonpositive.

Proof. Expand the mutual informations: \(I(A{:}B) + I(A{:}C) = 2S(A) + S(B) + S(C) - S(AB) - S(AC)\) and \(I(A{:}BC) = S(A) + S(BC) - S(ABC)\). The inequality \(I(A{:}B) + I(A{:}C) \le I(A{:}BC)\) rearranges to \(S(A) + S(B) + S(C) + S(ABC) \le S(AB) + S(AC) + S(BC)\), which is (2). The tripartite form is the same inequality moved to one side. ◻

Lemma 32 (Strong subadditivity from subadditivity and monogamy). For three single regions, strong subadditivity follows from subadditivity together with monogamy of mutual information. At \(n = 3\) the strong subadditivity family is therefore a consequence rather than an independent input. This argument covers three-region instances only: at \(n = 2\) monogamy is vacuous, and at \(n = 4\) the strong subadditivity instances involving four regions are not generated by three-region monogamy and remain independent inputs of 28.

Proof. The conditional mutual information factors as \(I(A{:}C \mid B) = I(A{:}BC) - I(A{:}B)\). Monogamy (2) in the form of 31 gives \(I(A{:}BC) - I(A{:}B) \ge I(A{:}C)\), and subadditivity gives \(I(A{:}C) \ge 0\). Hence \(I(A{:}C \mid B) \ge 0\), which is strong subadditivity for the triple. ◻

Lemma 33 (The witness satisfies every other standard inequality). The entropy vector of 29 is nonnegative, is zero on \(\emptyset\), and satisfies subadditivity, the Araki-Lieb bound, and strong subadditivity. It violates only (2). Hence the independence of 29 is not caused by any other failure.

Proof. All entries are \(0\) or \(1\), so nonnegativity holds and \(S(\emptyset) = 0\) by convention. Subadditivity reads \(1 + 1 \ge 1\) on any pair and \(1 + 1 \ge 1\) with the triple, and the Araki-Lieb bound reads \(0 \le 1\); both hold. Strong subadditivity holds by the case analysis of 29. Monogamy is the only violated inequality, by the computation there. ◻

The margin is worth recording numerically. On the witness vector the monogamy left side \(S(A) + S(B) + S(C) + S(ABC)\) equals \(4\) and the right side \(S(AB) + S(AC) + S(BC)\) equals \(3\), so the violation is \(1\) in entropy units, while every strong subadditivity slack is \(0\). The computational check of 7 reproduces these numbers from the joint distribution of \(X\), and the sampled classical experiments reproduce the same pattern: strong subadditivity never fails, monogamy fails on a positive fraction of draws.

Remark 34 (Open problem: entropy cone for six or more regions). The enumeration gap that [6] left at five regions has since been closed: the complete facet list for \(n = 5\) is known [16]. For fixed \(n \ge 6\) the number of independent RT inequalities is finite, but the facet lists are open, and for general \(n\) only one infinite family of inequalities is known [6]. No verified resolution of the \(n \ge 6\) gap was found in the sources reviewed (cutoff 2026-10-29).

Remark 35 (What the cone classifies). \(\mathcal{H}_n\) classifies entropy vectors, not spacetimes. A point of the cone records the entropies an RT surface assignment can produce for \(n\) regions; recovering a bulk geometry from a point of the cone is a reconstruction problem of a different kind, taken up in 9. The two problems share vocabulary and do not share theorems.

Reconstruction from probes

Reconstruction in the sense of 7 asks what relational data suffices to determine a geometry. A classical pattern governs such results: an object is recovered from its probes, as commutative algebras are recovered from their characters. The two spacetime instances of the pattern that are theorems follow, one classical and one quantum-information-theoretic.

Theorem 36 (Causal reconstruction of conformal geometry). Let \((M,g)\) and \((M',g')\) be time-oriented distinguishing spacetimes of dimension \(n \ge 3\) and let \(\varphi \colon M \to M'\) be a bijection preserving the chronological order: \(p \prec q\) if and only if \(\varphi(p) \prec' \varphi(q)\) for all \(p, q\). Then \(\varphi\) is a diffeomorphism and a conformal isometry. Consequently the chronological order determines the topology of \(M\) and the conformal class \([g]\) of the metric [8], [9].

Argument structure. The argument has two stages. First, the chronological order determines the topology: Malament shows that an order-preserving bijection between distinguishing spacetimes is a homeomorphism, using order arguments on chains of timelike curves rather than any metric. Second, a homeomorphism that maps timelike curves to timelike curves preserves the conformal class, and in dimension \(n \ge 3\) the causal, differential, and conformal structures of a spacetime determine each other, so the homeomorphism upgrades to a smooth conformal isometry [9]. The complete argument is Malament’s [8] with the smooth upgrade of [9]. In dimension \(n = 2\) the conclusion fails: in null coordinates \((u,v)\) on \(\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\) the map \((u,v) \mapsto (u^3, v)\) preserves the chronological order but its inverse is not smooth at \(u = 0\).

36 is the strongest reconstruction statement in this volume, and its scope is exactly the scope of 7: the probe datum is the chronological order, the class is the distinguishing spacetimes, and the equivalence is the conformal isometry class. The next corollary records the boundary of the theorem.

Corollary 37 (The order does not fix the conformal factor). No reconstruction of a metric from the chronological order alone can determine the conformal factor: distinct metrics \(g\) and \(e^{2f} g\) on the same manifold carry the same chronological order and are not isometric unless \(f\) is constant. Recovering the conformal factor requires additional probe data, such as a volume form, a time function normalized by matter, or stress-energy data.

Proof. Conformal rescaling of a metric by a positive function \(e^{2f}\) preserves the timelike cones of every tangent space, hence preserves the chronological order. Distinct conformal factors give distinct metrics with the same probe datum, so no procedure reading the order can distinguish them. Any reconstruction of the full metric must add data that breaks the conformal freedom. ◻

Two unrelated topologies share the name Alexandrov in English transliteration. The open-diamond topology on a spacetime, due to A. D. Alexandrov, is generated by the sets \(I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)\) and is sensitive to causality; the up-set topology on a poset, due to P. S. Alexandroff, is generated by up-sets and is generally not \(T_1\). The two are distinguished by name throughout what follows.

Lemma 38 (The order determines the diamond topology). The chronological order of a spacetime determines the open-diamond topology of A. D. Alexandrov: the sets \(I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)\) form a basis whose lattice structure is a function of the order alone. This diamond topology coincides with the manifold topology exactly when the spacetime is strongly causal [2], so on a strongly causal spacetime the order determines the topology of \(M\) without reference to the metric.

Proof. Each diamond \(I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)\) is defined by two order relations, so the family of diamonds is a function of the order, and the intersection of two diamonds is a union of diamonds. The identification of the diamond topology with the manifold topology under strong causality is the standard characterization recorded in the causal-hierarchy survey [2]. For merely distinguishing spacetimes the coincidence can fail, which is why 36 uses Malament’s order-continuity argument instead of this topological shortcut. ◻

Proposition 39 (Finite orders recover their order from their topology). Let \((X, \le)\) be a finite order and give \(X\) the up-set topology of P. S. Alexandroff, whose open sets are the up-sets. Then \(X\) is \(T_0\), the closure of a singleton is its down-set \(\mathrm{cl}(\{x\}) = \{ y : y \le x \}\), and the order is read off the topology by \(x \le y\) if and only if \(x \in \mathrm{cl}(\{y\})\).

Proof. The closed sets of the up-set topology are the down-sets. The closure of \(\{x\}\) is the intersection of all down-sets containing \(x\), which is the smallest such down-set \(\{y : y \le x\}\). For \(T_0\), take \(x \ne y\). Not both \(x \le y\) and \(y \le x\) hold. If \(x \not\le y\) then \(X \setminus \{z : z \le y\}\) is an up-set containing \(x\) and not \(y\); if \(y \not\le x\) the symmetric set separates \(y\) from \(x\). In either case an open set distinguishes the pair. The order is then recovered from the closures as stated. ◻

39 is the order-theoretic analogue of the topological step in 36, but it is not the same topology: 38 concerns the open-diamond topology of A. D. Alexandrov on a spacetime, while 39 concerns the up-set topology of P. S. Alexandroff on a poset. In both cases a topology built from the order recovers the order. What 21 blocks is the next step, the passage from order to geometry; that passage uses smoothness in 36 and has no finite-order analogue.

Theorem 40 (Entanglement-wedge reconstruction). In the holographic correspondence, let \(A\) be a boundary subregion of a code subspace of states with a smooth classical dual, and let \(W(A)\) be its entanglement wedge. Bulk operators supported in \(W(A)\) can be reconstructed as operators acting on \(A\) alone, within the code subspace. The result is a theorem of quantum information theory: its hypotheses are stated in the language of quantum error correction and quantum relative entropy, and its proof combines the relative-entropy argument for subregions with the error-correcting structure of the correspondence [11].

Argument structure. The argument has two inputs. First, the quantum relative entropy of the subregion’s state equals a bulk relative entropy in the wedge, which fixes the modular Hamiltonian data of the bulk region. Second, the error-correcting properties of the code subspace allow bulk operators in the wedge to be represented on \(A\) without disturbing states outside the code subspace. Combining the two gives a boundary representation of each bulk operator in \(W(A)\), with the hypotheses of the cited source [11].

Remark 41 (Layers). 40 is a quantum-information theorem and 36 is a differential-geometric theorem. Neither is a theorem of gravity. 40 speaks of a code subspace in a holographic correspondence; the interpretation of that code subspace as a theory of quantum gravity is the conjectural layer of 10. Keeping the layers apart is the discipline that makes the claims checkable.

Probe data recovers a space only where distinct spaces produce distinct probe structures. The classical instance is the Gelfand representation theorem: a compact Hausdorff space is recovered up to homeomorphism from its commutative unital \(C^*\)-algebra of continuous functions, through the Gelfand spectrum of characters in the weak-* topology. 36 is the spacetime instance, recovering conformal geometry in dimension \(n \ge 3\) from chronological order, and 40 is the quantum-information instance, representing wedge operators on boundary subregions. Category theory names the general pattern: the Yoneda embedding characterizes an object by the totality of its probes. The pattern alone establishes nothing physical. The qualification binding this program is stated once here and applies throughout: Yoneda does not, by itself, establish physical equivalence, empirical adequacy, or a classification theorem.

The conjectural core

Everything in this section is a proposal or an argument. Nothing here is used as a premise of a theorem elsewhere in the paper.

Remark 42 (The Ryu-Takayanagi proposal). The Ryu-Takayanagi prescription [10] proposes that the entanglement entropy of a boundary region \(A\) equals the area of a minimal bulk surface homologous to \(A\), in units of \(4G_N\): \[ S(A) = \min_{\gamma_A \, \sim \, A} \frac{\mathrm{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4 G_N}, \] where the minimum runs over bulk surfaces \(\gamma_A\) homologous to \(A\) in the spatial slice. The proposal is argued from the AdS/CFT correspondence. Exact agreement holds in the AdS\(_3\)/2D CFT case of the original statement [10] and for spherical entangling surfaces in arbitrary dimension, where a conformal mapping argument derives the formula [17]; a replica derivation for static bulk geometries explains the prescription at the physics level [18], and a covariant extremal-surface generalization is proposed in [14]. None of these arguments is a theorem of constructive quantum field theory, and the prescription remains a proposal. The holographic entropy cone of 28 is defined through this prescription, so the completeness of that theorem is a completeness statement about the cone of a proposal. What is proved there is the combinatorial enumeration of the inequalities the proposal produces in the classical limit; what is not proved is that the prescription holds in any given theory.

Remark 43 (Toy models). The HaPPY codes [12] are exactly solvable tensor-network models built from perfect tensors. Inside these models the Ryu-Takayanagi formula and the negativity of tripartite information hold exactly, and bulk logical operators admit multiple boundary representations that mimic wedge reconstruction. The models prove statements about tensor networks. They are not evidence about actual AdS/CFT beyond the analogy they are designed to exhibit, and no result of this paper treats them as evidence about gravity.

Remark 44 (Entanglement and connectedness). Van Raamsdonk’s essay [13] argues that the emergence of classically connected spacetime is tied to quantum entanglement: disentangling the degrees of freedom of two regions makes the regions pinch off from each other, quantified by standard entanglement measures. The status of the statement is an argument based on examples, not a theorem. It states a research direction for reconstruction: if entanglement builds connectedness, then entanglement data should determine geometric data. 36 shows what such a determination would have to look like as a theorem, with class, datum, and equivalence declared. No such theorem is claimed here.

The gap between these remarks and a reconstruction theorem of quantum gravity is not a technicality. 36 recovers a conformal class from a causal order; it does not derive a metric from entanglement. 40 recovers operators inside a code subspace; it does not derive the code subspace from first principles. A derivation of spacetime geometry from informational data without assuming the geometry at the start would need both steps in reverse order and is not on the shelf.

Remark 45 (What an upgrade would require). To promote the entanglement-connectedness argument [13] to a theorem of the kind 7 asks for, three things would be needed: a probe datum consisting only of entanglement data on a declared family of subsystems, a proof that the datum determines a spacetime up to a declared equivalence, and a demonstration that the construction does not smuggle in the geometry at the start. 36 shows the second item is achievable when the probe datum is a causal order and the equivalence is conformal. 37 shows that even then a conformal factor remains undetermined, so the third item requires either additional data or a theorem that the factor is physically irrelevant. None of the three items is established in the sources reviewed (cutoff 2026-10-29).

def ssa_holds(ent):
    # Strong subadditivity S(U) + S(V) >= S(U+V) + S(U*V).
    for u, v in itertools.product(range(8), repeat=2):
        lhs = ent[u] + ent[v]
        rhs = ent[u | v] + ent[u & v]
        if lhs + 1e-12 < rhs:
            return False
    return True

def mmi_holds(ent):
    # Monogamy S(A)+S(B)+S(C)+S(ABC) <= S(AB)+S(AC)+S(BC).
    A, B, C = 1, 2, 4
    lhs = ent[A] + ent[B] + ent[C] + ent[A | B | C]
    rhs = ent[A | B] + ent[A | C] + ent[B | C]
    return lhs <= rhs + 1e-12

def check_witness():
    # Perfectly correlated triple A = B = C = X, X a uniform bit.
    ent = [0.0] * 8
    for m in range(1, 8):
        ent[m] = 1.0
    ssa = ssa_holds(ent)
    mmi = mmi_holds(ent)
    assert ssa and not mmi
    return ssa and not mmi

def has_increasing_loop(le, n):
    # Strictly increasing loop x0 < x1 < ... < x0 (real test).
    strict = [[le[x][y] and x != y for y in range(n)] for x in range(n)]
    for start in range(n):
        stack = [(start, frozenset({start}))]
        while stack:
            node, seen = stack.pop()
            for nxt in range(n):
                if not strict[node][nxt]:
                    continue
                if nxt == start:
                    return True
                if nxt not in seen:
                    stack.append((nxt, seen | {nxt}))
    return False

def is_distinguishing(le, n):
    # Past- and future-distinguishing (Definition 2.3).
    for x in range(n):
        for y in range(n):
            if x == y:
                continue
            px, fx = past_future(le, x, n)
            py, fy = past_future(le, y, n)
            if px == py or fx == fy:
                return False
    return True

Limitations

Every proved result here classifies a narrow target rather than spacetime itself. The holonomy classification [1] classifies holonomy groups and not spacetimes. The causal ladder [2] classifies causality conditions and not geometries. Geometrization [3], [4] classifies closed 3-manifolds, and 25 shows there is no algorithmic analogue in dimension four. The entropy cone theorem 28 classifies entropy vectors produced by a proposal. No theorem in this paper classifies Lorentzian spacetimes themselves up to isometry, and no such theorem is claimed.

The holographic material is a proposal in all of its parts. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula has exact checks in special settings [10], [17] and a physics-level replica derivation for static bulk geometries [18], and it is not a theorem. The HaPPY codes prove statements about toy models [12]. The entanglement-connectedness statement is an argument [13]. Every result of this paper that touches holography is stated conditionally on the prescription, and the conditionality is written where the result is used.

The reconstruction theorems recover different objects. The causal theorem 36 recovers a conformal class from an order. The entanglement-wedge theorem 40 recovers operators inside a code subspace from boundary algebras. Neither recovers a metric from entanglement data, and the distance between these results and a derivation of gravity from information is a research program, not a step.

The Python experiments test finite instances of the propositions proved in 29, 20, and 21; the Haskell instances type the objects of 8. The evidence is reproducible from the files in the appendix and is not proof. Nothing in this volume has been checked by a proof assistant, and the mechanization targets are recorded separately as targets. This volume depends on the shared definitions and on the registered background results it cites. It does not depend on any statement about the entropy cone for six or more regions, on the Ryu-Takayanagi formula outside its stated settings, or on tensor-network toy models as evidence about gravity; where a result needs those inputs, the conditional phrasing appears in the statement.

Conclusion

Spacetime admits classification exactly where its invariants are finite or parametric and reconstruction exactly where probes determine a target up to a stated equivalence. Lorentzian holonomy groups form a classified list with realizing metrics [1]. Causality conditions form a complete ladder [2]. Closed 3-manifolds form a finite table of eight geometries [3], [4], and closed 4-manifolds do not admit algorithmic recognition [5]. The holographic entropy cone is a complete polyhedral calculation for at most four regions [6], probed here by a witness showing that its two inequality families are independent (29).

Reconstruction has one proved classical form: the chronological order determines the topology and the conformal class [8], and the conformal factor is exactly the undetermined freedom (37). It has one proved quantum-information form: bulk operators in an entanglement wedge are boundary-representable in the code subspace [11]. Between them lies the conjecture that entanglement determines geometry [10], [12], [13], presented here with its status on every appearance.

The negative results are part of the answer. The causal ladder rungs depend on their translation to finite orders (22), so a discrete quantum-gravity model that uses its rungs must state its definitions and prove its own causality theorems. Monogamy of mutual information is not free from strong subadditivity (29), so from three regions on the entropy cone genuinely needs both inequality families. Dimension four has no algorithmic classification (25), so a classification chart of this kind has rows that end in impossibility results rather than in lists. Each of these is a boundary, and each boundary narrows the targets a classification can honestly claim.

Four open problems delimit the next stage. The complete RT facet lists for six and more boundary regions are unknown; five regions are settled [16], and for general \(n\) only one infinite family of inequalities is known [6]. The invariants of 1 do not separate isometry classes (12), and which additional global invariants complete such a classification is open. The conformal factor left undetermined by 37 is either physically irrelevant or recoverable from additional probes, and the choice is not made by any theorem here. A determination theorem for spacetime from entanglement data would need the three items of 45, and none is established in the sources reviewed (cutoff 2026-10-29). The classification of Lorentzian spacetimes up to isometry remains, as this paper has tried to make precise, exactly where its invariants stop.
