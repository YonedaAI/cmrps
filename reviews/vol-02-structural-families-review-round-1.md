### Overall Evaluation

This manuscript proposes a ten-family taxonomy for mathematical representations of physical systems, paired with a formal discipline for recording classifying invariants, classified sectors, and impossibility obstructions. Separating parameterization, exhaustive classification, algorithmic recognition, normal forms, and obstruction theory is a sound methodological goal. However, the manuscript is not publishable in its present form. Several of its central mathematical statements and proofs are false or internally contradictory, its foundational definitions leave morphisms and compositions of system records unspecified, its survey of the ten families omits major theorems while misplacing others, its bibliography relies predominantly on Wikipedia and nLab entries rather than primary sources, and its prose routinely violates the house style through meta-commentary, nominalization, and flat paratactic splicing.

1. **Mathematical Correctness:** Two of the four headline results contain fatal errors or broken hypotheses. Proposition 16.1 (`\label{prop:closure}`, lines 515–522) conflates a class being *closed* under direct sum with a class *generating* a closure under direct sum, yielding a false conclusion contradicted by the paper's own finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra sector. Theorem 3.2(3) (`\label{thm:sharpness}`, line 125) assumes $\mathfrak{F}/\!\simeq$ is finite—making constructibility trivial and contradicting both line 53 and Corollary 14.1 (`\label{cor:wild}`, line 468)—while omitting algebraic closure of the ground field; Corollary 14.1 itself fails because Definition 2.1 (`\label{def:family-record}`, line 66) places no regularity condition on $I$. Supporting proofs also fail in Proposition 2.4 (`\label{prop:components}`, lines 86–93), Proposition 3.6 (`\label{prop:kinds}`, lines 172–181), Theorem 7.1 (`\label{thm:calg}`, lines 317–324), Proposition 5.2 (`\label{prop:recognition-cost}`, lines 283–290), Proposition 12.2 (`\label{prop:kraus}`, lines 432–439), and Proposition 18.1 (`\label{prop:mixed}`, lines 564–571).
2. **Clarity:** Undefined project-internal artifacts ("the ontology of the series", "the founding text", "the periodic chart", "the corpus", "the typed taxonomy layer") appear throughout without citation or self-contained definition, preventing the paper from standing on its own.
3. **Completeness:** Several family surveys omit landmark results directly relevant to their stated scope: Family I omits Moser's stability theorem, Gromov non-squeezing, symplectic capacities, and Floer/Gromov–Witten invariants; Family III omits Freedman's topological classification and Donaldson/Seiberg–Witten smooth obstructions in dimension four; Family VII names "operator semigroups" in its title without mentioning Hille–Yosida, Lumer–Phillips, or GKSL/Lindblad generators; Family X devotes nine lines to derived field theories without discussing BV/AKSZ formalisms or factorization algebras. Appendix A omits any URL, repository, or archive location for the accompanying code despite line 55 promising to record "where it lives".
4. **Logical Structure:** Statements in the text repeatedly contradict adjacent text or tables: the Abstract announces three governing results (line 44) whereas the Introduction and Conclusion announce four (lines 53, 658); Proposition 3.6 proves four achievement kinds are exhaustive for *any* sector (line 174), yet line 181 places sectors with no record outside the four kinds; line 112 refers to the "fourth column of every family table", though four of five tables have only three columns; line 606 claims the tables below give "one row per sector where the sectors differ", whereas Table 5 (`\label{tab:families}`, lines 639–648) gives one row per family; and the Introduction roadmap (line 55) omits four of the paper's twenty-one sections.
5. **LaTeX Quality:** Every `tabularx` environment lacks an `X` column so column widths sum to $0.90\textwidth$ beneath full-width `booktabs` rules; five preamble packages (`graphicx`, `xcolor`, `tikz`, `longtable`, `listings`) go unused; `\subsubsection` is invoked directly under `\section` without an intervening `\subsection` (lines 390, 471), numbering headers `10.0.1` and `14.0.1`; math symbols (`C*`, `*`, `II1`, `III0`, `G2`, `E8`) appear in text mode; `=` sits outside math delimiters on line 367; and backslash escapes inside `\url{...}` (lines 688–790) print literal backslashes and break hyperlinks.
6. **Prose Quality (House Style):** Across the manuscript, single-sentence throat-clearing paragraphs announce upcoming results (lines 117, 144, 315, 360, 376, 430, 600), family sections open with verbless fragments (`Representative systems: ...`), paragraphs bundle unrelated claims (line 464), qualifications precede findings (line 51, line 397), and adversative relationships (`but`, `whereas`) are flattened into mechanical `and`-coordinations (lines 49, 136, 139, 236, 299, 441, 450, 464).

### Weakest Three Paragraphs and Model Rewrite

#### 1. Lines 51–51 (Section 1, Paragraph 2)
> This paper works at the level where that distinction bites first. Before any volume can classify physical systems, it needs a vocabulary of the mathematical families capable of representing them, a record of what is classified inside each family, and a discipline for choosing the data that identification actually runs on. Those are the three deliverables here. The families are recorded as candidates, their classified islands and their obstructions are recorded with the status the literature supports, and the invariant discipline is stated as a set of definitions and results that later volumes can instantiate.

*Critique against house style:* The paragraph opens with vague metaphor and meta-signposting ("works at the level where that distinction bites first"), places a subordinate prerequisite clause ("Before any volume can...") ahead of the main claim, relies on colloquial and managerial filler ("bites first", "actually runs on", "Those are the three deliverables here"), and repeats the same three-item list twice in consecutive sentences, the second time entirely in the passive voice with stacked relative clauses ("with the status the literature supports", "that later volumes can instantiate").

#### 2. Lines 55–55 (Section 1, Paragraph 4)
> Section \ref{sec:defs} fixes notation and the record structures. Section \ref{sec:discipline} states the invariant discipline and the sharpness criterion. Sections \ref{sec:family1} to \ref{sec:family10} treat the ten families in turn. Section \ref{sec:exceptional} treats exceptional structures and their failure to close. Section \ref{sec:independence} treats independence and reconstructability. Section \ref{sec:limitations} records what the taxonomy cannot support. Section \ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. An appendix records the computational evidence and where it lives.

*Critique against house style:* This paragraph is pure structural signposting with no substantive claim, reasoning, or evidence; under the house style it should be deleted outright. Worse, it is factually inaccurate: it omits Sections 4 (`\label{sec:worked}`), 5 (`\label{sec:morphisms}`), 18 (`\label{sec:mixing}`), and 19 (`\label{sec:interface}`), and Appendix A never states where the code lives.

#### 3. Lines 464–464 (Section 14, Family IX, Paragraph 2)
> The proved results are regime-restricted and the impossibility results are not. Lattice gauge results hold in restricted regimes and do not by themselves close a continuum limit. In the wild direction, rank-two isomorphism classes of maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over the generic determinant admit no parameterization by a finite-dimensional variety \cite{buchweitz}, and compact equivariant modules over a cyclic group of prime order at an odd prime are wild \cite{grevstad}. A companion boundary is algorithmic: an isomorphism test running in polynomial time exists for groups that are tame extensions, and the boundary between the tame and wild regimes is an algorithmic boundary as well \cite{grochow}.

*Critique against house style:* The paragraph lacks a single unifying claim, stitching together uncited lattice gauge continuum remarks, commutative algebra (`\cite{buchweitz}`), equivariant stable homotopy theory (`\cite{grevstad}`), and group isomorphism complexity (`\cite{grochow}`) inside a section nominally devoted to discrete lattice and network models. It flattens contrast into `and` ("are regime-restricted and the impossibility results are not"), uses signposting transitions ("In the wild direction,", "A companion boundary is algorithmic:"), substitutes participial nominalizations for direct verbs ("an isomorphism test running in polynomial time exists"), and drops the ring spectrum $H\underline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ from `\cite{grevstad}`, making the mathematical statement ill-typed (or false if read as $\mathbb{F}_p[C_p]$-modules, which have finite representation type).

#### Model Rewrite of Paragraph 1 (Line 51)
> Classifying physical systems requires a candidate list of representing mathematical families, a scoped inventory of known classifications and obstructions within each family, and a strict criterion for what counts as a classifying invariant. Without named families, equivalent presentations of one system cannot be compared across formalisms; without explicit scope limits and complete invariants, normal forms and local coordinates are easily mistaken for global classifications. The ten families and invariant records below supply this framework without assuming that the family list is exhaustive or mutually exclusive.

### Structured Feedback by Severity

#### Critical Issues

1. **False headline non-closure deduction (Abstract line 44; Introduction line 53; Proposition 16.1, lines 515–522; Limitations line 604; Conclusion line 658).** Proposition 16.1 proves that if $\mathcal{E}$ consists of nonzero objects indecomposable under direct sum and $\mathcal{K}$ contains direct sum, then $\mathcal{E} \subsetneq \langle \mathcal{E} \rangle_{\mathcal{K}}$ (assuming $\mathcal{E} \neq \emptyset$, which is omitted from the hypotheses on line 517). From $\mathcal{E} \neq \langle \mathcal{E} \rangle_{\mathcal{K}}$, line 517 concludes: *"Consequently a generating family for the closure $\langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{K}}$ must contain composite objects and cannot consist of irreducibles alone"* (and line 44 states *"collections of irreducible objects cannot generate a family under direct sum"*). This confuses *being closed* under $\mathcal{K}$ with *generating* a closure under $\mathcal{K}$. By the definition of closure on line 79, $\mathcal{E}$ itself generates $\langle \mathcal{E} \rangle_{\mathcal{K}}$; in Theorem 7.1 (`\label{thm:calg}`, line 319), the simple matrix algebras $\{M_n(\mathbb{C}) : n \ge 1\}$ consist solely of $\oplus$-indecomposables and generate all finite-dimensional unital complex $C^*$-algebras under $\oplus$, while $\{\mathbb{C}\}$ generates all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces under $\oplus$. In addition, "nonzero" and "irreducible under direct sum" are never defined in the category of system records, and Table 2 (`\label{tab:exceptional}`, lines 504–506) conflates *exceptional/sporadic* objects with *simple/indecomposable* objects (the direct sum of *any* two simple Lie algebras or von Neumann factors fails to be simple or a factor, whether exceptional or regular).
2. **Broken hypotheses in the Sharpness Criterion and false Corollary 14.1 (Introduction line 53; Theorem 3.2(3), lines 125, 131; Corollary 14.1, lines 466–469).**
   - Line 125 assumes `$\mathfrak{F}/\!\simeq$ is finite`. Any finite subset of an algebraic variety is already a finite union of points (hence closed and constructible in standard varieties) without requiring $W$, regularity of $I$, or Chevalley's theorem; meanwhile, this finiteness hypothesis prevents applying Theorem 3.2(3) to infinite moduli problems such as Corollary 14.1 (`\label{cor:wild}`, line 468) and contradicts line 53. Evidently "finite" on line 125 was meant to modify the type of the morphism $W \to V$, not $|\mathfrak{F}/\!\simeq|$.
   - Even with `$\mathfrak{F}/\!\simeq$ is finite` removed, Chevalley's theorem yields constructibility of the image on $k$-rational points $W(k) \to V(k)$ only over an *algebraically closed* field $k$ (over $\mathbb{R}$, $x \mapsto x^2 \colon \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{R}} \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{R}}$ has image $[0,\infty)$, which is semialgebraic, not Zariski-constructible). No ground field is specified, and "regular on a variety $W$ that surjects onto $\mathfrak{F}/\!\simeq$" confuses a set-map $I \colon \mathrm{Ob}(\mathfrak{F}) \to V$ with an algebraic morphism compatible with flat/algebraic families parametrized by $W$.
   - Corollary 14.1 (line 468) claims that a sector admitting no parameterization by a finite-dimensional variety *"admits no invariant-complete record whose range is a finite-dimensional variety."* Because Definition 2.1 (line 66) defines $I \colon \mathrm{Ob}(\mathfrak{F}) \to V$ as an arbitrary map of sets with no algebraicity or Borel regularity condition, any sector with at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ classes injects set-theoretically into $\mathbb{A}^1(\mathbb{C})$. Wildness excludes *algebraic family-compatible* invariants, not bare set-theoretic maps.
3. **Contradiction in Achievement Kinds and invalid binary-choice proof (Definition 3.5, lines 167–170; Proposition 3.6, lines 172–181; Table 1, lines 183–199).** Proposition 3.6 states: *"For any sector $\mathcal{S}$ exactly one achievement kind applies. The four kinds are pairwise exclusive and exhaustive"* (line 174). Two sentences after the proof ends, line 181 states: *"Sectors with no record at all, such as the dynamical and semigroup family, sit outside the four kinds because nothing is classified there"*, and Table 1 adds a fifth row (`No record`, line 196). Definition 3.5 conflates mathematical existence of an invariant with epistemic presence of a theorem in the literature, leaves "required cardinality" (line 169) undefined as part of the input data of $\mathcal{S}$ (without target constraints, $V = \mathcal{S}/\!\simeq$ always provides a tautological classifying invariant), and falsely asserts in lines 169 and 178 that the four kinds arise from *"two independent choices"* when the proof actually branches along three nested conditional splits ($1 + 1 + 2 = 4$).
4. **Flawed proof of Proposition 2.4 (`\label{prop:components}`, lines 86–93).** The proposition assumes *each* of the five components of $P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})$ carries its own invariant data, and claims the 5-tuple of component invariants need not classify $P$. That statement is true only because component-wise invariants miss *inter-component coupling* (e.g., two inequivalent actions of the same group $\mathcal{S}$ on the same algebra $\mathcal{O}$). Yet the proof on line 92 argues instead that fixing $X$ and $\mathcal{O} = M_2(\mathbb{C})$ leaves dynamics $\mathcal{D}$ undetermined, concluding *"The tuple of component invariants therefore misses the dynamical and compositional data"*—forgetting that $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{C}$ *are* components in the 5-tuple!
5. **Flawed statement and proof sketch in Theorem 7.1 (`\label{thm:calg}`, lines 317–324).** On line 319, the invariant range $V$ of the sector is stated as multisets *"of total sum at most $\dim A$"*, leaving the codomain $V$ dependent on a bound object variable $A$ (for the sector of all finite-dimensional unital $C^*$-algebras, $V$ is countably infinite; only at fixed dimension $d = \sum n_i^2$ is the range finite). In the proof sketch (line 323), *"The C*-identity forces each $D_i$ to equal $\mathbb{C}$, since a finite-dimensional division algebra carrying a C*-norm admits no nonzero nilpotents and is commutative"* is mathematically wrong on three counts: every finite-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ equals $\mathbb{C}$ solely because $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed (no $C^*$-norm is needed); every division ring already has no nonzero nilpotents by definition; and a real $C^*$-division algebra need not be commutative ($\mathbb{H}$ is a real $C^*$-algebra). Conversely, the proof omits the two places where the $C^*$-structure actually does work: forcing the Jacobson radical to vanish so Artin–Wedderburn applies, and upgrading an algebra isomorphism to a $*$-isomorphism.
6. **Missing equivalence relation and conflation of map equality with isomorphism in Proposition 12.2 (`\label{prop:kraus}`, lines 432–439).** Despite line 62 requiring every classification statement to fix which equivalence relation is in force, Proposition 12.2 specifies none. Its proof (line 438) tests strict *equality* of maps $\Phi_U = \Phi_{U'}$ on $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ to obtain parameter space $PU(2)$. Under two-sided channel isomorphism $\Psi = \beta \circ \Phi \circ \alpha^{-1}$ with $\alpha, \beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(M_2(\mathbb{C}))$, every unitary channel $\Phi_U = \mathrm{Ad}_U$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{id}_{M_2(\mathbb{C})}$, collapsing the classes to a *single point*! Under simultaneous unitary conjugacy $\mathrm{Ad}_V \circ \Phi_U \circ \mathrm{Ad}_V^{-1} = \Phi_{VUV^*}$ (endomorphism isomorphism), the classes are conjugacy classes in $PU(2) \cong SO(3)$, parameterized by the rotation angle $[0, \pi]$, not $PU(2)$.
7. **False geometric-quantization claim in Proposition 18.1 (`\label{prop:mixed}`, lines 564–571).** Line 570 asserts that *"A prequantum line bundle over a symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$ carries a connection whose algebra of parallel sections is noncommutative once the polarization is not real, and the group of symplectomorphisms preserving the data is a Lie group."* First, prequantum line bundles exist only when $[\omega/(2\pi\hbar)]$ is integral. Second, polarized (covariantly constant along a polarization) sections of a line bundle $L \to M$ form a *vector space* (whose completion is the quantum Hilbert state space), not a noncommutative $C^*$-algebra—sections of $L$ cannot even be multiplied within $\Gamma(L)$ since $L \otimes L = L^{\otimes 2}$ (the noncommutative $C^*$-algebra consists of bounded *operators* on the polarized Hilbert space, or Berezin–Toeplitz operators on a compact Kähler manifold).
8. **Missing computability hypothesis in Proposition 5.2 (`\label{prop:recognition-cost}`, lines 283–290).** Line 285 states: *"If $V$ has decidable equality and the list of attained values is finite and stored, recognition is an algorithm."* Decidable equality on a finite set $V$ (such as $\{0, 1\}$) does not make recognition algorithmic unless *computing $I(P)$ from a presentation of $P$* is itself computable—a hypothesis omitted from the proposition (though acknowledged informally on line 292).
9. **Unscholarly reliance on tertiary wikis and misattributed citations (lines 49, 454, 493, 602, 685–792).** Twenty-three of the thirty-five bibliography entries are Wikipedia or nLab pages (`\cite{cfsg}`, `\cite{atlas}`, `\cite{lie}`, `\cite{hw}`, `\cite{darboux}`, `\cite{poisson}`, `\cite{vnalg}`, `\cite{calg}`, `\cite{stinespring}`, `\cite{qchannel}`, `\cite{dr}`, `\cite{deligne}`, `\cite{fusion}`, `\cite{haagkastler}`, `\cite{dhr}`, `\cite{classifying-space}`, `\cite{charclass}`, `\cite{anomaly}`, `\cite{tqft}`, `\cite{gelfand-naimark}`, `\cite{gelfand-duality}`, `\cite{stone}`, `\cite{adyanrabin}`, `\cite{geometrization}`). Line 49 cites Wikipedia (`\cite{cfsg}`) for a verbatim quote from Gorenstein; line 454 cites the nLab Doplicher–Roberts page (`\cite{dr}`) for Etingof and Gelaki's theorem on isocategorical finite groups (*Int. Math. Res. Not.*, 2001); line 493 cites `\cite{geometrization,tancer}` for Filipkiewicz's classification of 4D model geometries, which Tancer does not treat; and line 602 cites Wikipedia (`\cite{cfsg}`) for claims about *"The founding text"* of the author's own series. Primary peer-reviewed sources (Connes 1976, McDuff 1969, Perelman 2002–2003, Chentsov 1972, Weinstein 1983, Doplicher–Roberts 1989/1990, Deligne 2002, Etingof–Gelaki 2001, Conway et al. 1985, Stinespring 1955, Milnor–Stasheff 1974) must replace wiki entries throughout.

#### Major Issues

1. **Undefined categorical foundations and admissible constructions (lines 60–80, 257–266).** Morphisms of 5-tuple system records $P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})$ are never defined, leaving "the category of representations" (line 60), "isomorphism of the record" (line 62), "full subcategory" (line 66), and "coarse-grainings" / "embeddings" (line 261) mathematically ungrounded. Likewise, the admissible constructions $\mathcal{K}$ (line 76: tensor product, direct sum, extension, limits, colimits, factorization, quotient) are never defined on 5-tuples; on line 211, the *product* of symplectic manifolds is called *"the direct sum of systems with the product symplectic form"*.
2. **Worked examples violate the paper's own invariant discipline and contain concrete errors (Section 4, lines 203–237; Section 10.0.1, lines 390–400).**
   - Line 206 promises that Sections 4.1–4.3 each name *"one invariant, one range, one equivalence, and one scope"*, yet none of the three specifies all four fields.
   - Section 4.1 (`\label{sec:cotangent}`, lines 211–222) provides no classifying invariant: homotopy type of $Q$ is an invariant of $T^*Q$, not a complete symplectomorphism invariant (e.g., homotopy-equivalent lens spaces $L(7,1)$ and $L(7,2)$ do not have symplectomorphic cotangent bundles), and ignores the Hamiltonian $H$. Line 221 contains the tautology *"A homotopy equivalence is a homotopy equivalence"* and the garbled clause *"compatible with the composition of the $t$ parameter up to homotopy through the same formula"*.
   - Section 4.2 (`\label{sec:worked-calg}`, line 227) states that the state space of $A = M_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus \mathbb{C}$ is *"the set of density matrices on $\mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{C}$"*. Density matrices on $\mathbb{C}^2 \oplus \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C}^3$ form the state space of $M_3(\mathbb{C})$; states on $M_2(\mathbb{C}) \oplus \mathbb{C}$ correspond strictly to *block-diagonal* density matrices $\rho_2 \oplus p$. Moreover, claiming $\{2,1\}$ classifies the full record (line 229) ignores the dynamics $\mathcal{D}$ included on line 227, contradicting Proposition 2.4 (line 92).
   - Section 4.3 (`\label{sec:worked-bundle}`, line 234) omits dynamics $\mathcal{D}$ from the 5-component record and conflates classifying topological bundles $P \to S^2$ with classifying spaces of connections $\mathcal{A}(P)$.
   - Section 10.0.1 (`\label{sec:binary}`, lines 397–398) asserts that a binary statistical sector including Markov-kernel dynamics has a single-point classifying invariant range by Chentsov's theorem, directly contradicting Remark 10.2 (line 387: *"It is not a classification of classical statistical systems"*). A single-point classifying range implies all systems in the sector are equivalent regardless of their Markov kernel.
3. **Uncountability vs. Borel anti-classification in operator algebras (lines 169, 195, 328–333).** Line 328 deduces impossibility of classifying separable type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors solely from uncountability via Theorem 3.2(4). Yet quantum monotone metrics (line 383) and $PU(2)$-channels also form uncountable ($2^{\aleph_0}$) continua while being treated as parametrically classified. McDuff's continuum of $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors rules out *countable* invariants; genuine impossibility of smooth/parametric classification rests on descriptive set-theoretic non-smoothness / non-classifiability by countable structures (Hjorth; Sasyk–Törnquist), which is absent from the paper's framework.
4. **Domain misplacements and missing qualifier in Family IX (lines 464–469).** Maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over generic determinantal rings (`\cite{buchweitz}`) and compact modules over the equivariant Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum $H\underline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ (`\cite{grevstad}`) belong to commutative algebra and equivariant stable homotopy theory (Family X), not discrete lattice and network models (Family IX). On line 464, dropping "$H\underline{\mathbb{F}}_p$" in *"compact equivariant modules over a cyclic group of prime order at an odd prime are wild"* makes the claim false if read as modules over the group ring $\mathbb{F}_p[C_p] \cong \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t-1)^p$, which has *finite* representation type.
5. **Internal contradictions in cross-references, counts, and tables (lines 44, 53, 55, 112, 136–139, 160, 367, 606, 639–648).**
   - Abstract line 44 says *"Three results"* vs. line 53 and line 658 *"Four results"*.
   - Line 112 claims *"The fourth column of every family table below is this discipline applied"*, whereas Tables 1–4 have only three columns and Table 5 puts `Classifying invariant` in column 3 and coarse `Status` labels in column 4.
   - Corollary 3.3 (line 136) assumes a *"finite classifying invariant"* (finite $V$ per Theorem 3.2(2)), then line 139 immediately applies it to finite simple groups, which have *infinitely* many isomorphism classes.
   - Line 148 (`\label{prop:globality}`) omits $n \ge 1$ (for $n = 0$, connected $0$-dimensional symplectic manifolds are points, so a constant invariant *does* classify them) and overlooks that dimension $2n$ itself is a local germ invariant across varying dimensions.
   - Line 367 claims a principal-bundle sector is `invariant-complete` *"When the base and the group are such that the homotopy set is finite or algebraic"*, contradicting Definition 2.2 (line 71), which imposes no finiteness or algebraicity condition on $V$, and contradicting the very next sentence applying Theorem 3.2 (whose hypothesis is `invariant-complete`) to arbitrary $V = [X, BG]$.
   - Line 606 states *"The tables below therefore give one row per sector where the sectors differ"*, directly contradicted by Table 5 (`\label{tab:families}`, lines 639–648), which assigns a single row to each of the ten families.
6. **Conjecture wrapped in `\begin{theorem}` and unreferenced series dependencies (lines 60, 257, 306, 408–416, 513, 557, 573, 620).** Wrapping the Cobordism Hypothesis—explicitly labeled `"claimed"` and `"an informal sketch"`—inside `\begin{theorem}` (`\label{thm:cobordism}`, lines 408–411) misuses the theorem environment; use a `Conjecture` or `Claim` environment, and cite proved low-dimensional cases mentioned on line 415 (e.g., Schommer-Pries for $n=2$). Similarly, "logically independent" families (lines 53, 548–557, 620) is never formally defined, making the open problem on line 620 ill-posed. Part 1 of the series and all external project artifacts ("the ontology", "the founding text", "the periodic chart", "the typed taxonomy layer", and the code repository in Appendix A) must be properly cited or removed in favor of self-contained definitions.
7. **Broken `tabularx` layouts and `\url` escaping in LaTeX (lines 15–16, 188, 500, 534, 615, 635, 673, 688–790).** None of the six `\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{...}` tables contains an `X` column; each specifies fixed `L{...}` columns summing to $0.90\textwidth$, causing `booktabs` horizontal rules to overhang the columns across `\textwidth`. In the bibliography (lines 688, 694, 697, 700, 703, 706, 709, 712, 715, 718, 724, 772, 790), escaping `\_`, `\*`, and `\%` inside `\url{...}` prints literal backslashes in the output PDF and breaks every affected hyperlink.
