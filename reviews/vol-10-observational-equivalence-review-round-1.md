## Overview Evaluation

This manuscript (Part 10 of a larger series on classifying mathematical representations of physical systems) proposes a functorial definition of observational equivalence, contrasts classical and quantum information-geometric distinguishability via monotone Riemannian metrics, and formulates admissibility constraints on candidate notions of physical equivalence. While the overarching distinction among mathematical isomorphism ($\cong$), physical equivalence ($\simeq$), and functorial observational equivalence ($\approx_{\mathrm{Obs}}$) is worthwhile, the manuscript is not publishable in its current form. Several central mathematical claims, proofs, and worked calculations contain substantive errors, key definitions remain uninstantiated or depend on unpublished internal documents, cross-references and table numbers are broken, and the prose violates core requirements of the house style.

### Summary by Evaluation Criterion

1. **Mathematical Correctness:** Deficient in several core sections. Theorem 5.2 omits Petz's self-reciprocity condition $f(x) = x f(x^{-1})$, making its uniqueness statement ("distinct $f$ give distinct metrics") false for Riemannian metrics on self-adjoint tangent vectors; Example 5.4 and Section 9.2 use $f(x) = x$, which violates that symmetry condition. Section 5.4's adopted formal construction applies Lemma 4.3 to metric-threshold relations, overlooking that $d(\rho, \sigma) \le \varepsilon$ is non-transitive on connected state spaces for $\varepsilon > 0$ (hence cannot arise from any observation functor by Proposition 4.2) and collapses to state equality independently of $f$ at $\varepsilon = 0$. Section 11 (line 531) inverts the set-theoretic monotonicity of intersections $\bigcap_{\mathrm{Obs} \in \mathcal{A}} \approx_{\mathrm{Obs}}$. Section 4.2 contains a categorical type error ($\mathrm{Obs} \circ c$ for a morphism $c \colon P \to cP$), a vacuous second clause in Proposition 4.5, and a contradiction between lines 59 and 240. Proposition 4.6 cites Section 9.4 as a composite-system counterexample when Section 9.4 involves no composite systems. Section 9.1 (line 427) miscomputes the dimension of the fine equivalence classes on $\Delta_3$, claiming they are singleton points when they are $1$-dimensional line segments. Theorem 3.1 misstates Etingof–Gelaki's construction by attributing the group data to a non-trivial associator rather than a non-symmetric monoidal twist breaking the symmetric braiding.
2. **Clarity:** Undermined by undefined distinctions (e.g., "monoidal structure without symmetry" vs. "plain tensor structure" in Definition 2.5 and Table 2; "when the observation functor respects $c$" in Definition 2.6), conflation of strict functor fibers with essential fibers (lines 59, 63, 490, 555), and an unqualified outcome-relabeling requirement (line 403) that contradicts the worked examples in Sections 9.1 and 9.4.
3. **Completeness:** The manuscript is not self-contained. It relies throughout on undefined internal ontology codes (`DEF-002`, `DEF-008`–`DEF-018`, `DEF-042`, `T-OB-02`, `T-BG-14`, `T-BG-18`, `DEP-008`), cites unpublished internal documents not in the bibliography (`Founding document`, `YonedaAI research summary`), cites *nLab* wiki pages in half of its bibliography instead of primary mathematical literature (omitting Etingof–Gelaki, Davydov, Doplicher–Roberts, Chentsov, Stone, and Gelfand–Naimark), leaves Row 1 of Table 3 unconstructed despite textbook dualities being readily available, and never instantiates its own five-tuple system schema $P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})$ in any worked example.
4. **Logical Structure:** Repetitive and out of deductive order. Definition 2.4 depends forward on Definition 4.1; Section 6.1 depends forward on Sections 9 and 11. Gelfand/Stone/Gelfand–Naimark reconstruction and categorical supermaps are repeated nearly verbatim across Sections 3.2, 7, and 8; the four-word-identical "Yoneda qualification" sentence appears four times without attribution (lines 61, 185, 381, 397); and Section 10 re-narrates Section 9.
5. **LaTeX Quality:** Six unused packages are loaded (`graphicx`, `xcolor`, `tikz`, `longtable`, `listings`, `cleveref`). Despite loading `cleveref`, cross-references to tables and sections are hardcoded and broken: `Table 6` (line 461) and `Table 5` (line 488) are swapped; `Section 4.3` (line 125), `Section 5.3` (line 539), and the non-existent `Section 6.3` (line 539) are wrong. Every `tabularx` environment uses fixed-width `p{...}` columns with zero `X` columns, summing to $0.90\text{--}0.94\textwidth$.
6. **Prose Quality (House Style):** Despite aiming for a clipped declarative cadence, the prose repeatedly violates the house style through pure signposting and throat-clearing ("is worth recording", "deserves recording", full roadmap paragraph at line 65), qualification and bureaucratic framing ("The corpus records... and leaves it open") placed *before* claims, multi-claim grab-bag paragraphs (lines 59, 63, 125, 337, 393, 539), colloquialisms and metaphors ("has teeth", "proved killer", "rejected on sight", "uncharted strait between two proved islands"), repetitive internal jargon ("the corpus" appears 16 times), and paratactic `, and` splices where contrast (`but`) is meant.

### 2. Line 337 (Section 5.4)
> The first position: a single monotone metric defines distinguishability, and two states are separated when their metric distance exceeds a threshold. The classical case admits this reading (Theorem \ref{thm:chentsov}); the quantum case does not, by Corollary \ref{cor:noncanonical}. The second position: a family of thresholds, one per admissible metric, with the joint separation as the criterion. Lemma \ref{lem:products} applies to this construction, since a family of rulers is a product of observations, and the induced relation is the intersection of the per-ruler relations. This position is well defined and rests on nothing beyond Theorem \ref{thm:petzsudar}. Whether it is physically correct is a separate question: the intersection can be strict where a single ruler would identify, and no argument in the corpus picks the family over its members. The third position: operational distinguishability defined by the success of admissible experiments, with metrics as derived data rather than as the definition. This is the reading closest to the empirical layer (Section 8), and it is the least formalized: the admissible experiments of a general system class are not fixed.

**Why it fails the house style:** It packs three distinct positions and critiques into a single 176-word block rather than developing one claim per paragraph; withholds the author's conclusion until the next paragraph (line 339), violating "findings stated directly before qualification"; relies on verbless label fragments (`The first position:`, `The second position:`, `The third position:`), heavy nominalizations (`with the joint separation as the criterion`, `defined by the success of admissible experiments`), and insider filler (`no argument in the corpus`); and makes a false mathematical claim by applying Lemma 4.3 to non-transitive metric thresholds.

### 3. Line 379 (Section 7)
> Whether the two islands connect into one principle is open. The corpus records the question explicitly: whether the categorical-supermap line unifies with the Gelfand, Stone, and Naimark pattern into a single account of objects determined by their relationships is not established, and this paper does not close it. The relevance to observation functors is a hypothesis rather than a result. If representability of higher-order operations extends from supermaps to observation records in general, then records could be defined as representable objects and the functorial account of Section 4 would be derived rather than stipulated. The hypothesis needs a channel-state-duality statement for the systems in question, and no such statement is on record for general system classes.

**Why it fails the house style:** It spends three throat-clearing sentences (55 words) announcing that a question is open "in the corpus" and "is a hypothesis rather than a result" before finally stating the claim in sentence four, inverting "findings stated directly before qualification." It leans on metaphor (`the two islands`), bureaucratic phrasing (`The corpus records`, `on record`, `this paper does not close it`), nominalizations (`representability of higher-order operations`, `the functorial account`), and nested subordination (`whether the categorical-supermap line unifies with... into a single account... is not established`).

### Model Rewrite (of Line 379)
> Observation functors must be stipulated rather than derived because classical probe dualities and supermap representability do not yet unify across general physical systems. In theories admitting channel--state duality, higher-order operations are representable objects \cite{40,41}; if observation records were similarly representable across $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$, the functor $\mathrm{Obs}$ of Section~4 would follow from internal categorical structure just as Gelfand, Stone, and Gelfand--Naimark dualities recover spaces and algebras from probes \cite{42,43,44}. General system classes, however, lack an established channel--state duality, so no known theorem constructs observation records from morphisms of systems alone.

## Structured Feedback by Severity

### Critical Issues

1. **False uniqueness statement in Theorem 5.2 and invalid choice of $f(x) = x$ in Example 5.4 and Section 9.2 (lines 281–296, 311–320, 431–440, 575):**
   Theorem 5.2 asserts: *"Every quantum monotone metric arises from an operator monotone function $f$ with $f(1) = 1$ through the Morozova-Chentsov form, and distinct $f$ give distinct metrics."* When $g_f^\rho(A, A)$ is evaluated as a Riemannian metric on self-adjoint operators $A = A^*$ (line 292, where $|A_{ij}|^2 = |A_{ji}|^2$), this statement is **false**: Petz's classification requires the self-reciprocity (symmetry) condition $f(x) = x f(x^{-1})$. Without it, any operator monotone $f$ yields the exact same quadratic form on self-adjoint $A$ as its transpose $f^\#(x) = x f(x^{-1})$ and its harmonic symmetrization $\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}(f(x)^{-1} + (x f(x^{-1}))^{-1})\bigr)^{-1}$. Explicitly, $f_1(x) = x$, $f_2(x) = 1$, and $f_3(x) = \frac{2x}{1+x}$ are three distinct operator monotone functions on $(0, \infty)$ satisfying $f(1) = 1$, yet for every faithful $\rho$ and every self-adjoint $A$,
   \[
   g_{f_1}^\rho(A, A) = g_{f_2}^\rho(A, A) = g_{f_3}^\rho(A, A) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} |A_{ij}|^2 \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i} + \frac{1}{\lambda_j}\right).
   \]
   Moreover, $f(x) = x$ in Example 5.4 (line 313) and Section 9.2 (line 431) fails $f(x) = x f(x^{-1})$ (its associated complex sesquilinear form on $M_n(\mathbb{C})$ has a non-vanishing antisymmetric imaginary part on real tangent vectors). Add $f(x) = x f(x^{-1})$ to Theorem 5.2 (and $\mathrm{Tr}(A) = 0$ on line 292 to match the tangent-space condition on line 275), and replace $f(x) = x$ with $f(x) = \frac{2x}{1+x}$ throughout Example 5.4, Section 9.2, Section 10, and the Notation table; all numerical values ($16/3$ vs. $4$) remain unchanged while making Theorem 5.2 true.

2. **Invalid application of Lemma 4.3 to non-transitive metric thresholds in Section 5.4 (lines 335–340):**
   Lines 337–339 adopt as the paper's "formal construction" a family of metric thresholds, claiming that *"Lemma \ref{lem:products} applies to this construction, since a family of rulers is a product of observations, and the induced relation is the intersection of the per-ruler relations."* For any positive threshold $\varepsilon > 0$ on a connected state manifold (classical open simplex or faithful density operators), the threshold relation $d(\rho, \sigma) \le \varepsilon$ is **not transitive**, whereas Proposition 4.2 proves that *every* relation induced by an observation functor is transitive; hence no positive metric threshold is induced by an observation functor, and Lemma 4.3 does not apply. (Even classically, Theorem 5.1 fixes the Fisher metric only up to an arbitrary positive scalar multiple, so a fixed numeric threshold $\varepsilon > 0$ is not canonical either.) Conversely, if $\varepsilon = 0$, every Morozova–Chentsov metric $g_f$ is positive definite, so $d_f(\rho, \sigma) = 0 \iff \rho = \sigma$ for *every* $f$, falsifying the claim on line 337 that *"the intersection can be strict where a single ruler would identify."*

3. **Set-theoretic monotonicity reversal in Section 11 (line 531):**
   Line 531 claims: *"From above, the intersection of the observational relations can collapse: when the admissible family $\mathcal{A}$ contains enough observation functors, the intersection can identify systems that no working physicist would call the same."* This reverses elementary set theory and contradicts Lemma 4.3: adding observation functors to $\mathcal{A}$ **shrinks** $\bigcap_{\mathrm{Obs} \in \mathcal{A}} \approx_{\mathrm{Obs}}$ ($\mathcal{A}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{A}_2 \implies \bigcap_{\mathcal{A}_2} \subseteq \bigcap_{\mathcal{A}_1}$), so a larger family $\mathcal{A}$ identifies *fewer* systems, never more. Over-identification from above occurs when $\mathcal{A}$ contains *too few* observation functors; enlarging $\mathcal{A}$ too far instead collapses the upper bound downward toward $\cong$, separating dual presentations.

4. **Categorical type error, vacuous claim, and internal contradiction on coarse-graining (lines 59, 122, 226–240):**
   - *Type error (line 227):* Definition 4.4 introduces $c \colon P \to cP$ as a single morphism of systems, then writes $\mathrm{Obs} \circ c$, composing a functor with a morphism rather than an endofunctor (while Proposition 4.5 subsequently applies $c$ to two objects $P, Q$ as if $c$ were an endofunctor).
   - *Vacuous claim (lines 232, 236):* The second claim of Proposition 4.5 (*"If $cP \cong cQ$ for a coarse-graining map $c$ and an observation functor $\mathrm{Obs}$, then $cP \approx_{\mathrm{Obs}} cQ$"*) is merely the statement $X \cong Y \implies X \approx_{\mathrm{Obs}} Y$ specialized to $X = cP, Y = cQ$; it uses no property of coarse-graining and ignores Definition 2.6's undefined phrase *"when the observation functor respects $c$"* (line 122).
   - *Direct contradiction (line 59 vs. line 240):* Line 59 states that *"observations made after coarse-graining cannot separate systems that finer observations already identify,"* whereas line 240 states the exact opposite for observing after coarse-graining: *"two systems with isomorphic fine records may have coarse-grained images that a fixed observation functor separates."*

5. **Unproved failure direction in Proposition 4.6 and misidentification with Section 9.4 (lines 245–252, 484):**
   Proposition 4.6 asserts that when a composite system admits a joint experiment not determined by factor records, $P_1 \approx P_2$ and $Q_1 \approx Q_2$ can fail to imply $P_1 \otimes Q_1 \approx P_2 \otimes Q_2$. Its proof (line 251) constructs no composite system, instead citing Section 9.4 as exhibiting the mechanism. Section 9.4 contains **no composite systems $P \otimes Q$ or factor marginals at all**—it computes the product $(e_1, e_2)$ of two experiments on a *single* four-state system (illustrating Lemma 4.3, where the combined record *is* the product of the individual records). Construct an explicit bipartite witness (for example, on $\Omega \times \Omega$ with $\Omega = \{1,2,3,4\}$, single-factor observation $e_1$ under which $p \approx_{e_1} q$, and joint diagonal event $\omega_1 = \omega_2$, which has probability $11/32$ under $p \times p$ versus $9/32$ under $p \times q$). Also fix the symbol collision in lines 246–247, where $P_1 \otimes P_2$ denotes one composite in sentence one and $P_1, P_2$ denote competing first factors in sentence two.

6. **False geometric characterization of fine equivalence classes in Section 9.1 (line 427):**
   Line 427 asserts that on $\Omega = \{1,2,3,4\}$ with $E = \{e_1, e_2\}$, *"the class of $p$ over the coarse record is the set of distributions with $p_1 + p_2 = \tfrac{3}{4}$, and the fine classes are its points."* The probability simplex $\Delta_3$ is $3$-dimensional; imposing $p_1 + p_2 = 3/4$ cuts out a $2$-dimensional region, and imposing $p_1 + p_3 = 5/8$ (from $e_2$) cuts out a **$1$-dimensional line segment**, not a point:
   \[
   p(t) = \bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} + t,\; \tfrac{1}{4} - t,\; \tfrac{1}{8} - t,\; \tfrac{1}{8} + t\bigr), \qquad t \in \bigl[-\tfrac{1}{8}, \tfrac{1}{8}\bigr],
   \]
   every point of which satisfies $e_{1*}p(t) = (3/4, 1/4)$ and $e_{2*}p(t) = (5/8, 3/8)$. Reducing the fiber to points requires adding the third partition $e_3 = \{\{1,4\}, \{2,3\}\}$.

7. **Misstatement of Etingof–Gelaki's construction and misattribution in Theorem 3.1 (lines 139–146, 584–586):**
   Line 145 states that the isocategorical counterexample *"equips representations of non-isomorphic groups with monoidal structure whose associator carries the group data the forgetful criterion drops."* In Etingof–Gelaki's theorem, both $\mathrm{Rep}(G)$ and $\mathrm{Rep}(H)$ carry their **standard** representation-theoretic tensor product with the trivial vector-space associator; the monoidal equivalence $(F, J) \colon \mathrm{Rep}(G) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Rep}(H)$ uses a non-symmetric Drinfeld twist $J$ on the functor's monoidal structure map, which fails to intertwine the **symmetric braidings**, not a non-trivial associator on $\mathrm{Rep}(H)$. Furthermore, despite the label `\label{thm:eg}`, Theorem 3.1 never cites Etingof and Gelaki (*IMRN*, 2001) or Davydov (*J. Algebra*, 2001), citing instead an *nLab* wiki page on Doplicher–Roberts (`\cite{12}`).

   Line 403 states that *"two records that differ only by the naming of outcomes must be isomorphic."* Applied literally to single-experiment outcome distributions in Section 9.1, $e_{2*}p = (5/8, 3/8)$ and $e_{2*}q = (3/8, 5/8)$ differ by permuting the two outcome labels $\{1, 2\}$, which would render $e_{2*}p \cong e_{2*}q$ and destroy the separation in Sections 9.1 and 9.4.
