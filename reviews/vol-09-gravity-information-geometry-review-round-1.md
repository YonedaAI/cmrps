## Overall Evaluation

This manuscript surveys the boundary between proved theorems in finite-dimensional information geometry and conjectural proposals linking quantum information to spacetime geometry and gravity, framed as Part 9 of a larger classification series. While its central methodological aim—separating theorems (Chentsov, Petz–Sudár, Gelfand/Stone duality, Dong–Harlow–Wall entanglement-wedge reconstruction, and holographic entropy cone inequalities) from proposals (Ryu–Takayanagi and emergent gravitational dynamics)—is sound, the manuscript is not publishable in its current form. The elementary calculations proved in the paper (Propositions 3.2, 4.2, 4.3, 5.1, 5.2, and 9.1) are standard textbook exercises, while the surrounding definitions, theorem statements, worked examples, and literature claims contain multiple outright mathematical and factual errors.

### Summary by Evaluation Criterion

1. **Mathematical Correctness:** Several definitions, theorem statements, proofs, and worked examples are mathematically false or ill-posed. In particular:
   - **Theorem 4.1 (lines 227–230)** misstates the Petz–Sudár theorem both by fixing a single dimension $d \ge 2$ (operator monotonicity of all matrix orders requires a coherent family of metrics $\{g^{(d)}\}_{d \ge 2}$ monotone across *all* finite dimensions) and by claiming unnormalized monotone metrics (Definition 2.5) correspond bijectively to *normalized* operator monotone functions ($f(1)=1$).
   - **Remark 4.5 (line 284)** states a false numerical identity, claiming the fidelity metric $g^{\mathrm{fid}}$ equals the classical Fisher value $0.1633$ on a qutrit diagonal tangent vector, whereas $g^{\mathrm{fid}} = \frac{1}{4} g^{\mathrm{sld}}$ gives $0.0408$—contradicting Definition 2.8 and Proposition 5.2.
   - **Section 8 (line 438)** misdefines tripartite information as $I(A:B:C) = I(A:B) - I(A:BC)$ (omitting $+ I(A:C)$), reducing $I(A:B:C) \le 0$ to ordinary strong subadditivity rather than monogamy of mutual information.
   - **Remark 8.5 (lines 449–452)** exhibits a purported "pure four-region state" with $S(D) = 2.0$, $S(A \cup B \cup C) = 4.5$, and $S(A \cup B \cup C \cup D) = 0$, violating purity ($S(ABC) = S(D)$) and the Araki–Lieb inequality $|S(ABC) - S(D)| \le S(ABCD)$ by $2.5$ units; no quantum or holographic state can have these entropies.
   - **Theorem 8.4 (lines 442–445)** falsely asserts in an October 2026 paper that the five-region holographic entropy cone is unknown (solved by Hernandez Cuenca in 2019) and that only one infinite family of holographic entropy inequalities is known; it also omits subadditivity and Araki–Lieb from the facet generators for $2 \le N \le 4$ regions without specifying purification.
   - **Definition 2.3 (lines 80–87), Proposition 3.2 (line 189), and Chentsov commentary (line 213)** leave $g_{\varphi p}$ undefined when $m \neq n$ or when $\varphi p \in \partial \Delta_{m-1}$, divide by $q_j = 0$ when $\varphi$ has a zero row, and falsely claim that permutation invariance is an independent hypothesis in Chentsov's theorem rather than an immediate consequence of monotonicity under invertible stochastic matrices.
   - **Definition 2.9 (lines 130–137) and Proposition 9.1 (lines 461–468)** require $S(U,V,N)$ to be "strictly concave in its extensive variables"—impossible by Euler homogeneity $S(\lambda U, \lambda V, \lambda N) = \lambda S(U,V,N)$, which makes the $3 \times 3$ extensive Hessian identically singular along $(U,V,N)$—and write an unphysical ideal-gas entropy missing the heat capacity $c_v$ and violating extensivity unless $c = 2$.

2. **Clarity:** Individual equations in Sections 3–5 are readable, but conceptual clarity suffers from conflated manifolds (identifying the open hemisphere $\mathcal{D}_2^\circ$ with the full three-sphere $S^3$ in the Abstract, Introduction, Remark 5.3, and Conclusion; calling the non-homogeneous orthant $\Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ "homogeneous" in Corollary 6.1), undefined series jargon ("Family V", "companion atlas", "claims-ledger matter", "weighted-nerve constructions"), and unstated curvature sign conventions.

3. **Completeness:** As an assessment of "gravity from information," the bibliography (16 entries, 3 of which are `nLab` wiki pages) omits foundational works central to its thesis: Jacobson (1995) on Einstein's equations as a thermodynamic equation of state; Lashkari et al. (2014) and Faulkner et al. (2014) deriving linearized Einstein equations around AdS from the entanglement first law; Lashkari et al. Worse, Remark 5.5 (line 335) cites a computational check of geodesic distance in Section 11 that does not exist in either Section 11 or [bures_qubit_geometry.py](file:///Users/mlong/Documents/Development/classification-physical-systems/src/vol-09-gravity-information-geometry/bures_qubit_geometry.py), and "weighted-nerve constructions" are discussed in five places (lines 45, 549, 589, 598, 614) without a single definition or citation.

## Structured Feedback by Severity

### Critical Issues

- **C1 (Lines 80–87, 94–100, 177–197): Ill-posed domain/codomain of monotone metrics and division by zero in Proposition 3.2.**
  Definition 2.3 defines monotonicity of a Riemannian metric $g$ on a single open subset $M \subset \Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ under arbitrary $m \times n$ stochastic matrices $\varphi$. When $m \neq n$, $\varphi p \in \Delta_{m-1}$ does not lie in the manifold where $g$ is defined; even when $m = n$, $\varphi$ need not map $M$ into $M$, and a stochastic matrix with an all-zero row (e.g., $\varphi = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, which satisfies $\varphi_{ji} \ge 0$ and $\sum_j \varphi_{ji} = 1$) sends $\Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ to $\partial \Delta_{m-1}$ where $q_j = 0$ and $g_{\varphi p}$ is undefined. In the proof of Proposition 3.2 (line 189), "Dividing by $q_j = \sum_i \varphi_{ji} p_i$" divides by zero whenever $q_j = 0$ (or simply omit the terms where $q_j = 0$, since $\varphi_{ji} = 0$ for all $i$ forces $(\varphi u)_j = 0$, and extend $g$ or restrict $\varphi$ to interior-preserving maps across a *family* $\{g^{(n)}\}_{n \ge 2}$). The exact same domain/rank defect appears in Definition 2.5 (lines 96–99), where a general channel $\Phi$ (e.g., reset to a pure state, or mapping $\mathcal{D}_d \to \mathcal{D}_{d'}$ with $d' \neq d$) leaves $\mathcal{D}_d^\circ$.

- **C2 (Lines 208–214, 96): False scope claim regarding permutation invariance in Chentsov's theorem.**
  Line 213 states: *"Two scope conditions are load-bearing. Permutation invariance is part of the hypothesis: without a symmetry requirement the monotone metrics form a wider set."* This is mathematically false. Every coordinate permutation $\Pi$ is a stochastic matrix whose inverse $\Pi^{-1}$ is also a stochastic matrix. Applying monotonicity (Definition 2.3) to $\Pi$ at $p$ and to $\Pi^{-1}$ at $\Pi p$ yields $g_{\Pi p}(\Pi u, \Pi u) \le g_p(u, u) \le g_{\Pi p}(\Pi u, \Pi u)$, so every monotone metric is *automatically* permutation-invariant. Likewise, in Definition 2.5 (line 96), unitary invariance is redundant because $\rho \mapsto U\rho U^\dagger$ is a quantum channel with channel inverse $\rho \mapsto U^\dagger \rho U$.

- **C3 (Lines 227–232, 255–258, 282–285): Two mathematical errors in Theorem 4.1 (Petz–Sudár) and a false calculation in Remark 4.5.**
  1. *Missing normalization on the metric side:* Definition 2.5 defines monotone metrics without scale normalization, so if $g$ is monotone then $c g$ is monotone for every $c > 0$. Theorem 4.1 claims monotone metrics correspond *exactly* to *normalized* operator monotone functions ($f(1) = 1$). That is false up to positive scale: $f(1) = 1$ classifies only monotone metrics normalized to equal the Fisher metric on diagonal tangents. This error directly causes two contradictions: Remark 4.2 (line 257) lists the fidelity metric $g^{\mathrm{fid}}$ as a member of the normalized Petz family ($f(1)=1$), even though $g^{\mathrm{fid}} = \frac{1}{4} g^{\mathrm{sld}}$ corresponds to $f(t) = 2(1+t)$ with $f(1) = 4$; and **Remark 4.5 (line 284)** asserts that on the qutrit diagonal tangent $Y = \operatorname{diag}(0.2, -0.1, -0.1)$ at $\rho = \operatorname{diag}(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)$, *"all three metrics [SLD, Wigner–Yanase, and fidelity] give the Fisher value $0.04/0.5 + 0.01/0.3 + 0.01/0.2 = 0.1633$."* In fact, $g^{\mathrm{fid}}(Y, Y) = \frac{1}{4}(0.163333) = 0.040833$ (as Proposition 5.2's own proof confirms on line 320!).
  2. *Wrong quantifier over dimension $d$:* Theorem 4.1 states *"For each $d \geq 2$ the monotone metrics on $\mathcal{D}_d^{\circ}$ correspond exactly to normalized operator monotone functions."* Petz (1996) classifies *coherent families* $\{g^{(d)}\}_{d \ge 2}$ across **all** dimensions $d \ge 2$ that are monotone under channels $\Phi : M_d(\mathbb{C}) \to M_{d'}(\mathbb{C})$. Fixing a single $d$ (e.g., $d=2$) and requiring monotonicity only under endomorphisms of $\mathcal{D}_d$ cannot force $f$ to be operator monotone of matrix orders $n > d$.

- **C4 (Lines 262–273): Proof defects in Proposition 4.2 (Commutative locus).**
  First, Proposition 4.2 is stated for all $d \ge 2$ and is applied to qubits ($d = 2$) in Remark 4.4 (line 279) and Proposition 5.2 (line 320), yet the Chentsov step in its proof (line 272) holds only for $d \ge 3$. Second, line 272 claims a classical stochastic map extends to a quantum channel by *"send $|i\rangle\langle i|$ to the diagonal state $\sum_j \varphi_{ji} |j\rangle\langle j|$ and extend linearly with complete positivity on the diagonal subalgebra."* Linear extension from $\{|i\rangle\langle i|\}_{i=1}^d$ defines a map only on the $d$-dimensional diagonal subalgebra, not on $M_d(\mathbb{C})$. One must compose with diagonal pinching: $\Phi(A) = \sum_{i,j} \varphi_{ji} \langle i | A | i \rangle |j\rangle\langle j|$ (Kraus operators $K_{ji} = \sqrt{\varphi_{ji}}|j\rangle\langle i|$).

- **C5 (Lines 438, 442–452): Wrong definition of tripartite information, impossible quantum state in Remark 8.5, and false theorem statement in Theorem 8.4.**
  1. *Line 438:* Defines tripartite information as $I(A:B:C) = I(A:B) - I(A:BC)$, omitting $+ I(A:C)$. The written expression is asymmetric in $(A,B,C)$ and equals $S(B) + S(ABC) - S(AB) - S(BC)$, so $I(A:B:C) \le 0$ as defined on line 438 is merely strong subadditivity, contradicting the correct MMI inequality $I(A:B) + I(A:C) \le I(A:BC)$ on line 451. Line 438 also omits the requirement that $A, B, C$ be pairwise disjoint.
  2. *Remark 8.5 (line 451):* Assigns to a *"pure four-region state"* the entropies $S(A)=S(B)=S(C)=S(D)=2.0$, two-region unions $3.5$, three-region unions $S(A\cup B\cup C)=4.5$, and $S(A\cup B\cup C\cup D)=0$. For **any** pure state on $ABCD$, $S(ABC) = S(D)$ identically; more generally, Araki–Lieb requires $|S(ABC) - S(D)| \le S(ABCD)$, which fails here ($2.5 \not\le 0$). These values correspond to a *mixed* four-region state purified by a fifth region $O$ with $S(ABCD) = S(O) = 5.0$ (realized, e.g., on $K_5$ with inter-region edge weights $0.25$ and purifier edge weights $1.25$), never to $S(ABCD) = 0$.
  3. *Theorem 8.4 (line 444):* States inside a `theorem` environment that *"For five and more regions only one infinite family of inequalities is known, and the complete inequality set is unknown."* Aside from placing historical ignorance inside a theorem statement, this is factually false: Hernandez Cuenca (*Phys. Rev. D* 100, 026004, 2019) proved the complete five-region holographic entropy cone, and multiple further infinite families of holographic inequalities (toric/projective-plane families, etc.) have been proved since 2019. In addition, without introducing the purifying region $O$, SSA and MMI alone do not generate the $N=2,3,4$ cones in $\mathbb{R}^{2^N-1}$: subadditivity $S(A)+S(B) \ge S(AB)$ and Araki–Lieb $S(AB)+S(A) \ge S(B)$ are also facet inequalities.

- **C6 (Lines 130–137, 461–475): Impossible strict concavity of extensive entropy in Definition 2.9 and unphysical ideal-gas entropy in Proposition 9.1.**
  1. *Definition 2.9 (line 132):* Assumes $S(U,V,N)$ is *"strictly concave in its extensive variables"* and defines $g^{\mathrm{rup}}_{ij} = -\partial^2 S / \partial X^i \partial X^j$ in extensive coordinates $X^i$. Because physical entropy is homogeneous of degree one, $S(\lambda U, \lambda V, \lambda N) = \lambda S(U,V,N)$, Euler's theorem gives $\sum_j (\partial^2 S / \partial X^i \partial X^j) X^j = 0$ (the Gibbs–Duhem relation). Thus the $3\times 3$ Hessian in $(U,V,N)$ has a zero eigenvalue everywhere and is *never* strictly concave or Riemannian; one must fix one extensive scale parameter (such as $N$, as done tacitly on line 463, or pass to densities).
  2. *Proposition 9.1 (line 463):* Writes the ideal-gas entropy as $S(U,V,N) = Nk(\ln U + \ln V - c\ln N + \text{const})$. The classical ideal gas has $S(U,V,N) = Nk(c_v \ln U + \ln V - (c_v + 1)\ln N + \text{const})$; setting the coefficient of $\ln U$ to $1$ forces $c_v = 1$ (contradicting the $a, b \to 0$ limit of the $c_v = 3/2$ van der Waals gas on line 474), and leaving $c$ arbitrary violates extensivity unless $c = c_v + 1$.

  Definition 2.10 defines $W(A)$ as *"the bulk domain bounded by $A$ and by the minimal surface homologous to $A$ in the bulk geometry."* In a $(d+1)$-dimensional Lorentzian asymptotically AdS spacetime, $A$ and $\gamma_A$ are $(d-1)$-dimensional (codimension-2) surfaces and cannot bound a spacetime domain; instead, $A \cup \gamma_A$ bounds a spatial homology hypersurface $\Sigma_A$ on a Cauchy slice, and $W(A) = D[\Sigma_A]$ is its causal domain of dependence, with $\gamma_A$ extremal (Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi) rather than Riemannian minimal outside static slices.

## Three Weakest Paragraphs and Model Rewrite

### 1. Remark 10.3 (Lines 515–518; body at line 517)
> Three natural counterexamples to the claims of this paper were sought and none survived. A stochastic map that strictly increases Fisher distinguishability is excluded by Proposition \ref{prop:monotone}.
