# Peer Review: *Foundations of Structural Classification* (Part 1 of *The Classification of Mathematical Representations of Physical Systems*)

## 1. Summary and Overall Assessment

This manuscript lays out the definitional and methodological foundations for a multi-volume program classifying mathematical representations of physical systems. It proposes a five-component system record $P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})$, defines five morphism kinds with concrete preservation laws, proves on a finite counterexample that those morphism kinds are not closed under composition (so the morphism vocabulary is a schema rather than a category), introduces six admissibility conditions defining $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$, demonstrates on finite classical and finite discrete-time dynamical systems how irreducibility depends on $\mathcal{C}$ and how complete classifications can be either parameterized ($\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$) or unparameterized (multisets of functional-graph component types), proves via a two-qubit rotation that tensor-factor decomposition is extra data beyond a bare Hilbert space, states the program's structural classification conjecture with five proof obligations, and establishes a six-tier claim-status discipline.

**Strengths.** The paper's greatest strength is its epistemic candor and structural discipline. Separating *classification*, *parameterization*, and *recognition* in Section 1; distinguishing mathematical isomorphism ($\cong$), declared physical equivalence ($\simeq$), and empirical adequacy in Section 5; stating the explicit *Yoneda qualification* (Remark 5.4); and enforcing a strict claim-status taxonomy that refuses to conflate computational tests or withheld formalizations with kernel-checked proofs (Section 10) set an unusually high standard of honesty for a foundational program. The counterexample in Proposition 4.4 and the contrast between Theorems 8.1 and 8.2 are clean, concrete, and well-chosen.

**Recommendation: Major Revision.** Despite its conceptual clarity, the manuscript currently has a **fatal LaTeX compilation error** (`example` is undefined), a **flawed proof** in Proposition 6.4, a **mathematical contradiction** around the monoidal unit and unique factorization in Section 7, an **incorrect universal quantifier** in Proposition 7.6 (`prop:factorization-nonunique`), a **mismatch between classical point-map preservation laws and quantum/duality records** in Section 4, a **misplaced citation and erroneous author initial** in the bibliography (alongside heavy reliance on Wikipedia/nLab in place of primary sources), and **factual mismatches between Appendix A and the companion code**. All of these issues are localized and cleanly repairable.

## 2. Critical / Blocking Issues

### C1. Fatal LaTeX compilation failure: `example` environment is never defined
In the preamble, `\newtheorem` declarations are given for `theorem`, `proposition`, `lemma`, `corollary`, `conjecture`, `definition`, and `remark`, but **no `\newtheorem{example}` declaration appears**. Neither `article` nor `amsthm` defines an `example` environment by default. Consequently, the four `\begin{example}` environments (`\label{ex:hamiltonian}`, `\label{ex:quantum}`, `\label{ex:union-irreducibles}`, `\label{ex:product-irreducibles}`) halt compilation with:
```text
! LaTeX Error: Environment example undefined.
```
*Fix:* Add `\newtheorem{example}[theorem]{Example}` under `\theoremstyle{definition}` in the preamble.

### C2. Flawed proof and unstated proposition in Proposition 6.4 (`prop:conditional`)
Proposition 6.4 asserts that statements quantified over $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ depend on the admissibility list, and attempts to prove this using AC-2 (*Observable closure*):
> *"Take condition AC-2 and the finite classical records. With AC-2 in force, $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{R}^{X}$ is an algebra and pullbacks of observables along coarse-grainings land in $\mathcal{O}$. With AC-2 dropped, records whose observable collection is a non-closed subset of $\mathbb{R}^{X}$ are admitted, and the pullback of two target observables can leave the collection, so the coarse-graining law of Definition \ref{def:concrete-laws} fails on an admitted record."*

There are three logical errors in this argument:
1. **Conflating $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ with $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$:** In $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ (Definition 3.4), $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{R}^X$ holds *by definition*, regardless of AC-2. Conversely, in $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ *with* AC-2 in force, AC-2 only requires $\mathcal{O}$ to be closed under its algebra operations—not equal to all of $\mathbb{R}^X$. For instance, $X_1 = \{1,2,3,4\}$ with constant functions $\mathcal{O}_1 = \mathbb{R}\cdot \mathbf{1}_{X_1}$ and $X_2 = \{p,q\}$ with $\mathcal{O}_2 = \mathbb{R}^{X_2}$ **both satisfy AC-2**, yet pullbacks along a surjection $\hat g \colon X_1 \twoheadrightarrow X_2$ already fail to land in $\mathcal{O}_1$.
2. **Confusing elementwise pullback with algebraic operations:** Pullback along $\hat g$ acts on *one* observable at a time ($O \mapsto O \circ \hat g$), not by combining two observables. Whether $O \circ \hat g \in \mathcal{O}_1$ depends on containment $\hat g^*(\mathcal{O}_2) \subseteq \mathcal{O}_1$, not on whether $\mathcal{O}_1$ is algebraically closed.
3. **No explicit universal sentence $\Phi$ is exhibited:** The proof never states a concrete sentence $\Phi$ quantified over $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ that is true with AC-2 and false without AC-2.

*Fix:* State an explicit property $\Phi$ over $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$—for example, *"For every $P \in \mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ with $\mathcal{O}(P) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^X$ and every $f, h \in \mathcal{O}(P)$, $f + h \in \mathcal{O}(P)$"* (or *"Whenever a surjection $\hat g \colon X_1 \to X_2$ pulls a generating set of $\mathcal{O}_2$ into $\mathcal{O}_1$, it pulls the subalgebra generated by that set into $\mathcal{O}_1$"*). With AC-2 in force, $\Phi$ holds on $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$; with AC-2 omitted, $X = \{1,2\}$ with $\mathcal{O} = \{\delta_1, \delta_2\} \subset \mathbb{R}^X$ is admitted and falsifies $\Phi$.

### C3. Undefined "nontrivial record" and unit-factor contradiction in Section 7 (`def:irreducible`, `def:decomposition`, `ex:product-irreducibles`)
Definition `def:irreducible` defines $P$ to be irreducible relative to $\mathcal{C}$ when it admits no decomposition $P \cong \mathcal{F}(P_1, P_2)$ with *"both $P_1$ and $P_2$ nontrivial records"*, without defining **nontrivial**. This creates an immediate contradiction between Examples `ex:union-irreducibles` and `ex:product-irreducibles`:
1. In `ex:union-irreducibles` (disjoint union on $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$), the one-point record $P_1$ ($|X| = 1$) **must count as nontrivial**, because $P_2 \cong P_1 \sqcup P_1$ is treated as a nontrivial decomposition making $P_2$ reducible.
2. In `ex:product-irreducibles` (Cartesian product on the *same* class of records), if $P_1$ counted as nontrivial, then $P_p \cong P_p \times P_1$ ($p = p \times 1$) and $P_1 \cong P_1 \times P_1$ ($1 = 1 \times 1$) would decompose every prime-cardinality record and $P_1$ itself, leaving **zero** irreducibles. Thus "nontrivial" must mean **not isomorphic to the monoidal unit of $\mathcal{C}$** (which is $\emptyset \notin \mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ for $\sqcup$, and $P_1$ for $\times$).
3. Worse still, `ex:product-irreducibles` states:
   > *"The irreducibles are the one-point record and the records of prime cardinality. A record of cardinality $n$ decomposes as a product of records of prime cardinality, and the multiset of prime factors is unique up to order by unique prime factorization. The unit is the empty product."*

   If the one-point record $P_1$ belongs to $\mathfrak{J}_{\times}$, then under Definition `def:decomposition` (*"$P \cong \mathcal{F}(I_1, \dots, I_k)$ with each $I_i \in \mathfrak{J}_{\mathcal{C}}$"*), every record has infinitely many decompositions of different lengths:
   $$P_6 \cong P_2 \times P_3 \cong P_2 \times P_3 \times P_1 \cong P_2 \times P_3 \times P_1 \times P_1 \cong \cdots$$
   destroying uniqueness of decomposition. Just as $1 \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ is a unit rather than a prime and the trivial group $\{e\}$ is excluded from the finite simple groups in CFSG, **an irreducible object relative to $\mathcal{C}$ must itself be non-unit** ($P \not\cong \mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}$) and admit no factorization into two non-unit objects. With that standard convention, $\mathfrak{J}_{\times}$ consists solely of the prime-cardinality records, $P_1$ is the empty product ($k = 0$), and unique decomposition holds unconditionally.

### C4. Incorrect quantifier in Proposition `prop:factorization-nonunique`
Proposition `prop:factorization-nonunique` reads:
> *"Fix a four-dimensional complex Hilbert space $H$ and two factorizations $H \cong \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$. There is a unit vector $\psi \in H$ whose entanglement entropy is $0$ in one factorization and $\log 2$ in the other."*

As stated, this universally quantifies over arbitrary pairs of factorizations ($\forall \Phi_1, \Phi_2 \, \exists \psi$), which is **false**: if $\Phi_2 = (U_1 \otimes U_2) \circ \Phi_1$ for $U_1, U_2 \in U(2)$, or $\Phi_2 = \mathrm{SWAP} \circ \Phi_1$, then **every** unit vector $\psi \in H$ has identical entanglement entropy in both factorizations. What the proof actually establishes is existential:
> *"For any four-dimensional complex Hilbert space $H$, **there exist** two factorizations $H \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$ and a unit vector $\psi \in H$ whose entanglement entropy is $0$ in one factorization and $\log 2$ in the other."*

Additionally, the final sentence writes *"The bare record $(H, B(H), \dots)$ does not determine the decomposition of $H$ into factors."* Do not hide $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{S}$ in `\dots`: write $(H, B(H), \mathrm{id}_H, U(H))$ explicitly, because if $\mathcal{S}$ were instead fixed as the concrete subgroup $U(2) \times U(2) \subset U(4)$ (as in Definition `def:qubit`), then $\mathcal{S}$ *would* pick out the tensor factorization up to factor swap.

### C5. Point-map bias in Definition 4.3 (`def:concrete-laws`) vs. Quantum Records, Duality, and Limiting Procedures
Definition 4.3 presents concrete preservation laws for all five morphism kinds, but three of them do not match the mathematical objects introduced in Sections 2–3:
1. **Quantum records vs. point-set state maps (laws 1 & 2):** In Example `ex:quantum` and Definition `def:qubit`, $X$ is a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{O} = B(\mathcal{H})$ consists of bounded linear operators on $\mathcal{H}$, **not** scalar functions on $\mathcal{H}$. For two qubits coarse-grained to one qubit, coarse-graining is given on density matrices by the partial trace $\mathrm{Tr}_B \colon \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B) \to \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}_A)$ and contravariantly on observables by the unital $*$-homomorphism $A \mapsto A \otimes I_B \colon B(\mathcal{H}_A) \hookrightarrow B(\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B)$—there is **no** surjective point map $\hat g \colon \mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B \to \mathcal{H}_A$ pulling operators back by precomposition $A \circ \hat g$. Likewise, realizing one qubit inside two qubits is a tensor factor inclusion on algebras rather than a linear subspace injection $\mathcal{H}_A \hookrightarrow \mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$. Definition 4.3 should explicitly clarify that laws (1)–(2) as stated are the *classical/point-set specialization*, or formulate them more generally via state–observable expectation pairings $\langle \omega, g^\sharp(O) \rangle = \langle g_*(\omega), O \rangle$.
2. **Duality (law 4 and Table 2):** Law 4 states *"The map satisfies the equivalence conditions after passage to the opposite record category"*, and Table 2 summarizes the state-level condition as *"Bijection after passage to an opposite"*. Mathematically, for any category $\mathbf{C}$, a morphism is invertible in $\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}$ **if and only if** it is already invertible in $\mathbf{C}$ ($\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}) = \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbf{C})$), and a set map $\hat g \colon X_1 \to X_2$ does not become a bijection by passing to an opposite category. Genuine dualities (Gelfand, Stone, Pontryagin, Kramers–Wannier) are equivalences $\mathcal{A} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}$ between *distinct* categories (or contravariant exchanges between state and observable layers), not state-space bijections in an opposite record category—especially since Corollary 4.5 shows no single record category has even been constructed yet.
3. **Limiting procedure (law 5) in the proof of Proposition 4.4 (`prop:composition-not-typed`):** Neither Definition 4.1 nor Definition 4.3(5) gives a formal mathematical axiom for the four named limit processes (thermodynamic, infinite-volume, semiclassical, continuum). Consequently, the final step in the proof of Proposition 4.4 (*"which act on directed systems of records and never produce a constant map between two fixed finite state spaces"*) appeals to informal physical knowledge outside Definition 4.3(5) (in $\mathbf{Set}$, constant maps between finite sets *can* arise as colimit comparison maps of finite diagrams, though not from $N \to \infty$ / $\hbar \to 0$ directed limits). Either add an explicit mathematical condition to Definition 4.3(5) (e.g., colimit/limit indexed by a non-stabilizing directed parameter set, excluding maps between two finite records in $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$) or state Proposition 4.4 for laws (1)–(4).

## 3. Important Mathematical and Technical Refinements

### M1. Weakly connected functional graphs and "irreducible" vs. "indecomposable" in Theorem 8.2 (`thm:dynamical-decomposition`)
1. **Specify *weakly* connected:** Because the functional graph $G_f = (X, \{(x, f(x))\})$ of $f \colon X \to X$ is a *directed* graph ("a directed cycle with rooted trees attached to its vertices"), "connected" is ambiguous between *weakly connected* (connectedness of the underlying undirected graph) and *strongly connected* (a pure cycle with no attached trees). Theorem 8.2 requires **weakly connected** throughout.
2. **Precision in the proof of Claim 2:** The proof writes: *"Any decomposition into irreducibles is a partition of $X$ into invariant subsets, and an invariant subset contains no crossing edge, so each irreducible factor is exactly one connected component."* Taken literally, a single invariant subset $Y \subsetneq X$ **can** have incoming crossing edges: on $X = \{1, 2\}$ with $f(1) = 1, f(2) = 1$, the graph $2 \to 1 \circlearrowleft$ is weakly connected, $Y = \{1\}$ is $f$-invariant ($f(Y) \subseteq Y$), and $(2, 1)$ crosses into $Y$. What prevents crossing edges is that **every block of the partition** is $f$-invariant: for any edge $(x, f(x))$, $x$ lies in some block $X_k$, and $f(X_k) \subseteq X_k$ forces $f(x) \in X_k$, ruling out outgoing (and hence all) edges between distinct blocks.
3. **Indecomposability vs. absence of invariant subsystems:** The same example $2 \to 1 \circlearrowleft$ highlights a valuable structural distinction worth noting in Section 7 or 8: $P$ contains a proper nonempty invariant subsystem $Y = \{1\}$ (Definition 3.5), yet admits no disjoint-union decomposition $P \cong P_1 \sqcup P_2$ because $X \setminus Y = \{2\}$ is not $f$-invariant. Thus Definition 7.1 defines **indecomposability** under $\mathcal{C}$ (analogous to indecomposable vs. simple modules when Maschke's theorem fails), rather than absence of proper subobjects.

### M2. Canonical state sets $\{1, \dots, n\}$ vs. general nonempty finite sets (`def:finite-classical`, `def:finite-dynamical`)
1. Definitions 3.4 and 3.5 restrict $X$ literally to $X = \{1, \dots, n\}$ ($n \ge 1$). However, Proposition 4.4 uses $X_1 = \{a,b\}$ and $X_3 = \{p,q\}$, Example `ex:union-irreducibles` restricts to $X \setminus \{1\} = \{2, \dots, n\}$, and Definition 3.5 restricts to arbitrary invariant subsets $Y \subseteq X$. Moreover, if $X$ must literally equal $\{1, \dots, n\}$, then $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ already has **only one object per integer $n$** prior to quotienting by $\cong$, rendering Theorem 8.1(3)–(4) tautological. Allowing $X$ to range over arbitrary **nonempty finite subsets of a fixed countable universe** (e.g., $\mathbb{N}$ or a Grothendieck universe, so that $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{fin}})/\cong$ is a genuine set rather than a quotient of a proper class) makes restrictions, disjoint unions, products, and $\{a,b\}$ literally belong to the class while keeping Theorem 8.1 nontrivial.
2. In Definition 3.5, require an invariant subsystem $Y \subseteq X$ to be **nonempty** ($Y \neq \emptyset$), since $n \ge 1$ excludes the empty state space from $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{dyn}}$.

### M3. Underlying datum of a "map of records" (Section 4 & Proposition 6.3) and coarse-graining details
1. Proposition 6.3 (`prop:schema-category`) proves associativity and unit laws by stating *"because the morphisms are maps of records and composition of maps is associative."* Yet Section 4 never defines what an untyped "map of records" $g \colon P_1 \to P_2$ actually is as a mathematical object (nor how two such maps compose) prior to imposing preservation laws! Define the underlying carrier explicitly (e.g., for concrete state-based classes, a map $\hat g \colon X_1 \to X_2$ inducing record transport, or a specified componentwise morphism tuple) so that composition and associativity are well-defined.
2. In Definition 4.3(1) (*Coarse-graining*), the third clause (*"and states of $X_1$ with the same image are identified by all pulled-back observables"*) is redundant if pullback means $O \mapsto O \circ \hat g$ (since $\hat g(x) = \hat g(x') \implies O(\hat g(x)) = O(\hat g(x'))$ automatically). Conversely, unlike Embedding (law 2) and Equivalence (law 3), law 1 imposes **no dynamical compatibility** (such as $\hat g \circ f_1 = f_2 \circ \hat g$ on $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{dyn}}$). Clarify whether coarse-grainings must intertwine dynamics or whether dynamical coarse-graining is deferred.

### M4. Impredicativity and escape clause in Admissibility Conditions AC-5 and AC-6 (`def:admissibility`)
1. **AC-5 (*Composition closure*):** Defining a single record $P$ to be *admissible* by requiring that *"when $\mathcal{C}$ is applied to admissible records, the result is an admissible record, or the record states the failure explicitly"* has two issues: (a) it is circular unless phrased as closure of the class $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ with respect to AC-1–AC-4, and (b) the escape clause *"or the record states the failure explicitly"* contradicts Table 3 (`tab:admissibility`), which states that AC-5 **excludes** *"A composition rule that produces inadmissible records"*. If partial composition is permitted when its domain of composability is explicitly declared as part of $\mathcal{C}$, state that formally as a domain specification on $\mathcal{C}$ and align Table 3 accordingly.
2. **AC-6 (*Declared equivalence*):** Neither $\simeq$ nor the morphism preservation laws is one of the five components of $P = (X, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C})$ (indeed, Definition 5.2 says $\simeq$ is declared *per class of systems*, and Table 3 lists *"Morphisms"* under the *"Component"* column even though Morphisms is not a component of $P$). Frame AC-6 cleanly as a requirement on an *admissible class/subcategory specification* $(\mathcal{K}, \mathrm{Mor}_{\mathcal{K}}, \simeq_{\mathcal{K}})$ rather than an intrinsic field of the 5-tuple $P$.

### M5. Mathematical inaccuracies in Table 1 (`tab:regimes`)
1. **3-manifolds:** Table 1 lists *"Closed manifolds up to homeomorphism"* with identifying data *"One of eight Thurston geometries"*. This conflates the classification of *model geometries* with the classification of *closed 3-manifolds*: first, a closed 3-manifold need not admit a single Thurston geometry (it undergoes Kneser–Milnor prime decomposition and JSJ torus decomposition into geometric pieces); second, naming one of the eight geometries does **not** identify a 3-manifold up to homeomorphism (infinitely many non-homeomorphic closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds and lens spaces share a single geometry).
2. **Principal $G$-bundles:** Aside from the misplaced citation `\cite{gelfand-naimark}` (see B1), the bijection $\mathrm{Prin}_G(B) \cong [B, BG]$ requires a **paracompact** Hausdorff base (or CW complex) $B$, and in the "Achievement" column *"Complete up to homotopy"* is misleading: bundles are classified **up to bundle isomorphism** by **homotopy classes** of classifying maps.

### M6. Unintroduced "ten structural families", notation collisions in Conjecture 9.2, and self-application of Section 10
1. **"The ten structural families":** Sections 8 (p. 7) and 11 (p. 9) refer to *"the ten structural families"* with the definite article, and Table 4's caption mentions *"a candidate family list"*, yet **the ten candidate families are never named or listed anywhere in the paper**. A reader of Part 1 alone has no referent for "the ten structural families." Add a brief remark in Section 9 naming the program's ten candidate organizing families (or drop the definite phrase "the ten structural families" in favor of "the candidate structural families of later volumes"). Also reconcile the Conclusion's *"open on four of its five obligations"* with the Abstract's *"none of them proved"* and Table 4's *"Partial"* status on Obligation 4 ("open on four obligations and partial on the fourth").
2. **Notation in Conjecture 9.2 (`conj:structural`):**
   - $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}} \simeq \langle \mathfrak{F}_\alpha \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$ has an unbound index $\alpha$ inside $\langle \cdot \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$, whereas Definition 7.3 defines $\langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$. Write $\langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$.
   - $\mathcal{C}$ is used both for the fifth coordinate of an individual record $P$ (Definition 3.1) and for the global collection of admissible constructions in Conjecture 9.2 (using $\mathfrak{C}$ for the global construction set, as in [`docs/theorem-registry.md`](file:///Users/mlong/Documents/Development/classification-physical-systems/docs/theorem-registry.md#L128-L130), would avoid the collision).
   - $\simeq$ denotes an equivalence relation between *individual records* in Definitions 5.2 and 9.1, but a relation between *classes/categories* in $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}} \simeq \langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$. State explicitly that $\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}} \simeq \langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{C}}$ means essential-image coincidence up to objectwise $\simeq$ (i.e., $\mathrm{Ob}(\mathrm{Phys}_{\mathrm{adm}})/\simeq \;=\; \mathrm{Ob}(\langle \mathfrak{F} \rangle_{\mathcal{C}})/\simeq$).
3. **Self-application of Claim-Status Labels (Section 10):**
   - Section 10 states: *"The series uses exactly six, and every theorem-level statement carries exactly one."* Yet none of the propositions, corollary, or theorems proved in Sections 4–8 explicitly displays its label in the paper. Adding a short ledger or parenthetical tag for Propositions 4.4, 6.3, 6.4, 7.6, Corollary 4.5, Theorems 8.1, 8.2, and Conjecture 9.2 would model the discipline directly.
   - Definition 10.1 defines *Complete-informal-proof* as *"a complete proof exists in the mathematical literature and the record cites it."* Broaden this wording slightly to *"a complete mathematical proof is given in full here or cited from the peer-reviewed mathematical literature"*, so that newly proved theorems of the series itself have a well-defined home among the six labels.

### B4. LaTeX `\*` bug in `\bibitem{cstar}`, raw URLs, and `tabularx` column specifications
1. **`C\*-algebra` bug in `\bibitem{cstar}`:** In LaTeX text mode, `\*` is a discretionary math-multiplication break command (`\discretionary{\thinspace\the\textfont2\char2}{}{}`) whose non-broken expansion is **empty**. Thus `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C\*-algebra` typesets as `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-algebra` (dropping the `*` completely!). Wrap all bibliography URLs in `\url{...}` (provided by `hyperref`, which is already loaded); this both preserves literal `*`, `_`, `%` characters without manual escaping and enables clean line breaks on long URLs.
2. **`tabularx` misused in all six tables & overflow in Table 6:** Every `tabularx{\textwidth}{...}` environment in Tables 1–6 uses only fixed `L{...}` (`p{...}`) columns summing to $0.90\textwidth$–$0.94\textwidth$ and **contains no `X` column**, defeating `tabularx` (triggering package warnings and misaligned table widths). Make the widest descriptive column `>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X`. Moreover, in Table 6 (`tab:code-evidence`), `\texttt{exp\_finite\_dynamics\_decomposition.py}` (36 monospace characters with no breakpoints) exceeds `L{0.32\textwidth}` at 10pt and overflows into the third column; use `\path{...}` (from `hyperref`/`url`) or widen Column 2.
