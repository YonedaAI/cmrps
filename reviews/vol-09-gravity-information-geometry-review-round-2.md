# Peer Review: *Gravity, Information, and Geometric Correspondence*

## 1. Evaluation by Criterion

- **Mathematical correctness:** All six primary calculations proved in [vol-09-gravity-information-geometry.tex](file:///Users/mlong/Documents/Development/classification-physical-systems/papers/latex/vol-09-gravity-information-geometry.tex)—Proposition 3.2 (Fisher monotonicity), Proposition 4.2 (non-proportionality of SLD and Wigner–Yanase metrics on qubits), Proposition 4.3 (restriction of normalized Petz metrics to the Fisher metric on commuting tangents), Propositions 5.1–5.2 (SLD and fidelity geometries of the Bloch ball), and Proposition 8.1 (flatness of the ideal-gas Ruppeiner metric)—along with Lemma 3.1 and all numerical values in Sections 3, 4, 7, 8, and 10 are algebraically and numerically accurate. However, several surrounding definitions, theorem statements, and remarks contain substantive mathematical defects: Definition 2.3 circularly defines monotonicity only for Fisher metrics, Theorem 3.3 omits the cross-dimensional family quantifier required for Chentsov's theorem (making the statement false as written at $n=2$), Definition 2.5 omits full-rank image hypotheses needed for Eq. (4) to be defined, Corollary 6.1 proves only one inclusion of its isometry-group claim, Remark 6.3 asserts a false claim about smooth non-Markov embeddings, Remark 4.5 applies a qubit-only proposition to a qutrit, and Theorem 7.1 omits the static-geometry hypothesis while misstating how subadditivity, Araki–Lieb, and five-party facets enter the holographic entropy cone.
- **Clarity:** Local exposition inside proofs is clean and explicit. Clarity suffers where series-internal terminology ("Family V of the series ontology," lines 58, 371, 376; unattributed direct quotation on line 415) appears without self-contained definitions or citations to earlier volumes, and where the Abstract contradicts the main text on the holographic entropy cone range ($N \leq 4$ on line 45 vs. $N \leq 5$ on lines 54, 446, 449, 598, 624).
- **Completeness:** The finite-dimensional calculations are classical results (Hübner 1992; Braunstein–Caves 1994; Petz 1996; Petz–Sudár 1996; Ruppeiner 1979) and need explicit attribution at the point of statement so expository proofs are not misread as claims of mathematical novelty. On the gravity–information side, two literature omissions undermine explicit "not on record" claims: Lashkari and Van Raamsdonk (*JHEP* 04 (2016) 153) proved that bulk canonical energy equals second-order relative entropy—selecting specifically $f_{\mathrm{bkm}}$ from the Petz family—while Miyaji et al. (*PRL* 115 (2015) 171602) studied holographic fidelity ($f_{\mathrm{sld}}$); and Faulkner et al. (*JHEP* 03 (2014) 051) and Lashkari, McDermott, and Van Raamsdonk (*JHEP* 04 (2014) 195) derived linearized Einstein equations around AdS from the Ryu–Takayanagi formula and the entanglement first law. Foundational theorems (Gelfand–Naimark and Stone duality, lines 386, 391) cite `nLab` wiki pages rather than primary literature or monographs.
- **Logical structure:** Sections 3–6 (monotone metrics on state spaces) and Sections 7–8 (probe reconstruction, Ryu–Takayanagi, and the entropy cone) run on largely separate tracks that Section 9 attempts to fuse by claiming (line 546; cf. line 50) that Ryu–Takayanagi proposes a mechanism turning state-space geometry (Row 1 of Table 1) into bulk spacetime geometry (Row 3). In fact, Ryu–Takayanagi takes von Neumann entropies $S(\rho_A)$ of spatial subregions of a *single* state—which carry no Petz parameter $f$—rather than a Riemannian metric on $\mathcal{D}_d^\circ$. Meanwhile, Definition 2.11 ("Weighted-nerve construction," lines 144–147) is introduced without mathematical specification or citation and is explicitly unused (line 613), and Remark 9.3 ("Counterexample search," line 519) interrupts Section 9 with elementary arithmetic checks from earlier sections.
- **LaTeX quality:** The preamble loads unused packages (`graphicx`, `xcolor`, `tikz`, `tabularx`, `longtable`, `listings`, and `cleveref`, lines 12–20); despite loading `cleveref`, all cross-references are hardcoded manually without `\cref`/`\Cref`. Every page range in the bibliography (lines 640, 681, 714, 720, 726, 738, 744, 750) and every multi-author compound name (`Wigner-Yanase`, `Ryu-Takayanagi`, `Petz-Sud\'ar`, `Kullback-Leibler`, `Cauchy-Schwarz`, `Gelfand-Naimark`, `Dong-Harlow-Wall`, `Araki-Lieb`) uses a hyphen `-` instead of an en-dash `--`. Non-terminating decimal approximations are equated to rational expressions with `=` rather than `\approx` (lines 207, 209, 284), natural logarithms switch from `\log` (Sections 3, 4, 7) to `\ln` (Section 8), and three bibliography entries lack closing periods (lines 693, 698, 703).
- **Prose quality (house style):** Many sentences achieve admirable economy, but paragraph discipline breaks down in transitional and synthetic passages. Several paragraphs bundle multiple distinct claims (lines 54, 56, 476), bury findings behind long qualifying preambles (line 476), rely on passive-voice roadmap signposting (`"The paper proves five results..."` on line 56; lines 341, 423, 486, 528), or lean on nominalizations (`"The reading of flatness is..."`, `"The suggestion ... is an argument"`, lines 54, 476, 519, 546).

### Major Issues

1. **Circular definition of classical monotonicity and false single-simplex statement of Chentsov's theorem (lines 45, 52, 79–86, 212–215, 270).**
   - In Definition 2.3 (line 81), monotonicity is defined only as *"a condition on the family $\{g^{(n)}\}$ of Fisher metrics across dimensions,"* and line 85 hardcodes the Fisher coordinate sum into the definition (*"the terms of $g^{(m)}_{\varphi p}$ over those coordinates are omitted"*). A general Riemannian metric $g^{(m)}$ on $\Delta_{m-1}^\circ$ has no coordinate-wise sum decomposition on $\partial \Delta_{m-1}$.
   - Conversely, Theorem 3.3 (line 214; also lines 45 and 52) states Chentsov's theorem on *"the interior of the probability simplex"* (singular) rather than for a coherent family $\{g^{(n)}\}_{n \geq 2}$ across dimensions (contrast Theorem 4.1 on line 231, which correctly specifies families $\{g^{(d)}\}_{d \geq 2}$). On a fixed simplex in isolation, uniqueness under stochastic endomorphisms fails at $n = 2$: the flat metric $g_p(u, u) = u_1^2 + u_2^2$ on $\Delta_1^\circ$ satisfies $g_{\varphi p}(\varphi u, \varphi u) = (a - b)^2(u_1^2 + u_2^2) \leq g_p(u, u)$ for every $2 \times 2$ stochastic matrix $\varphi = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ 1-a & 1-b \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ because $a, b \in [0, 1] \implies |a - b| \leq 1$. Cross-dimensional Markov embeddings $\Delta_1 \to \Delta_2$ are what eliminate $g^{\mathrm{flat}}$ and fix a single universal constant $c > 0$ across all $n \geq 2$. Define a classical monotone metric as a family $\{g^{(n)}\}_{n \geq 2}$ of Riemannian metrics on $\Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ satisfying Eq. (2) whenever $p \in \Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ and $\varphi p \in \Delta_{m-1}^\circ$, and state Theorem 3.3 for such families.

2. **Missing interiority hypotheses in Definition 2.5 (lines 93–100).**
   - Line 95 defines $\{g^{(d)}\}$ on full-rank states $\mathcal{D}_d^\circ$ only, yet quantifies Eq. (4) over *"every channel $\Phi : \mathcal{D}_d \to \mathcal{D}_m$, every state $\rho$."* If $\rho \in \partial \mathcal{D}_d$, $g^{(d)}_\rho$ is undefined; even if $\rho \in \mathcal{D}_d^\circ$, a channel such as the isometric block embedding $\Phi(\rho) = \rho \oplus 0 \in \mathcal{D}_{d+1}$ maps $\rho$ onto $\partial \mathcal{D}_{d+1}$ with $D\Phi \cdot X = X \oplus 0 \neq 0$, where $g^{(d+1)}_{\Phi(\rho)}$ does not exist. Restrict Definition 2.5 explicitly to $\rho \in \mathcal{D}_d^\circ$ and channels/states with $\Phi(\rho) \in \mathcal{D}_m^\circ$.

3. **Invalid application of a qubit-only proposition to qutrits and general $d$ (lines 127, 284, 314–321).**
   - Proposition 5.2 (`\ref{prop:qubit-fid}`, lines 314–321) states and proves $g^{\mathrm{fid}} = \tfrac{1}{4} g^{\mathrm{sld}}$ exclusively on $\mathcal{D}_2^\circ$, relying on the $2 \times 2$ matrix identity $(\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{M})^2 = \operatorname{Tr} M + 2\sqrt{\det M}$ (line 320). Yet Remark 4.5 (line 284, also a forward reference) divides $g^{\mathrm{sld}}$ by $4$ at a **qutrit** ($d=3$) off-diagonal tangent vector and writes *"(Proposition \ref{prop:qubit-fid} fixes that normalization)"* (note that [`qutrit_check`](file:///Users/mlong/Documents/Development/classification-physical-systems/src/vol-09-gravity-information-geometry/quantum_monotone_metrics.py#L85-L96) in [quantum_monotone_metrics.py](file:///Users/mlong/Documents/Development/classification-physical-systems/src/vol-09-gravity-information-geometry/quantum_monotone_metrics.py) does not compute fidelity either), and Definition 2.8 (line 127) concludes from the $d=2$ computation alone that $g^{\mathrm{fid}}$ is a scalar multiple of a Petz-family metric across dimensions. Either prove $g^{\mathrm{fid}} = \tfrac{1}{4}g^{\mathrm{sld}}$ for general $d \geq 2$ by expanding $\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}(\rho + \varepsilon X)\sqrt{\rho}}$ in the eigenbasis of $\rho$, or cite Braunstein and Caves `\cite{braunstein-caves}` (or Hübner 1992) for $d \geq 2$.

4. **Incomplete proof of Corollary 6.1 and false claim in Remark 6.3 (lines 343–350, 366).**
   - **Corollary 6.1 (lines 345, 349):** Line 345 asserts unconditionally that the Riemannian isometry group of $(\Delta_{n-1}^\circ, g^{\mathrm{fisher}})$ is the permutation group $S_n$. The proof (line 349) verifies only that coordinate permutations preserve the metric (*"the only global isometries induced by the simplex structure"*) and notes geodesic incompleteness. Geodesic incompleteness does not force a finite isometry group—the open hemisphere $(\mathcal{D}_2^\circ, g^{\mathrm{sld}})$ of Proposition 5.1 is also geodesically incomplete, yet its isometry group is $O(3)$. To prove $\operatorname{Isom}(\Delta_{n-1}^\circ, g^{\mathrm{fisher}}) \subseteq S_n$, note that any global isometry of the connected constant-curvature manifold $S_+^{n-1}(2)$ extends uniquely to an ambient isometry in $O(n)$ preserving the metric completion $\overline{S_+^{n-1}(2)}$ and therefore permuting its $n$ extreme rays.
   - **Remark 6.3 (line 366):** Asserts *"for embeddings that are not sufficient-statistic maps the intrinsic geometry does detect the embedding."* For smooth embeddings $i : M' \to \Delta_{n-1}^\circ$ in the sense of Definition 6.2 (lines 354–357), this is false: take any smooth non-geodesic arc inside $\Delta_2^\circ$ of total Fisher arc length $\pi$ (for example, a half-circle of Euclidean radius $1/2$ around $(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$ in $S_+^2(1)$) and parameterize it by $s(\theta) = 2\arcsin\sqrt{\theta}$ for $\theta \in (0, 1)$. The pullback metric is identically $d\theta^2 / (\theta(1-\theta))$, matching the Bernoulli Fisher metric both up to isometry and in coordinates, though $i$ is nonlinear and not a Markov map. What holds is that *among linear stochastic maps* $\varphi : \Delta_{k-1} \to \Delta_{n-1}$, equality $\varphi^* g^{\mathrm{fisher}} = g^{\mathrm{fisher}}$ holds if and only if $\varphi$ is a sufficient-statistic Markov embedding.

## 3. Three Weakest Paragraphs and Model Rewrite

### Weakest Paragraph 1 (line 54)

> The geometric side is mixed. Within quantum information theory, the entanglement-wedge reconstruction theorem of Dong, Harlow, and Wall \cite{dhw} is proved, and the holographic entropy cone is complete for at most five boundary regions \cite{bao, hc5}. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula \cite{rt} is argued from AdS/CFT; it is derived for spherical vacuum regions in any dimension \cite{chc} and for covariant extremal surfaces under stated assumptions \cite{lm, hrt}, and it remains a proposal for general regions and states. The suggestion that entanglement builds spacetime \cite{vr} is an argument. Thermodynamic geometry supplies the oldest working dictionary between information metrics and physical curvature: the Ruppeiner metric, the negative Hessian of entropy, is flat for the ideal gas and develops curvature in interacting models. The reading of curvature as an interaction diagnostic is supported by computed examples \cite{ruppeiner} and by the thermodynamic derivation of the Einstein equation of state \cite{jacobson}, and is a proposed correspondence beyond those settings.

- **House-style diagnosis:** Violates *one identifiable claim per paragraph* by combining two distinct research programs—holographic entanglement in AdS/CFT and equilibrium thermodynamic geometry—under a vague opener (`"The geometric side is mixed"`). Relies on passive constructions, nominalizations, and tautology (`"is argued from AdS/CFT"`, `"The suggestion that entanglement builds spacetime \cite{vr} is an argument"`, `"The reading of curvature as an interaction diagnostic is supported..."`), while misattributing covariant derivations to `\cite{hrt}` and conflating Jacobson's horizon equation of state with Ruppeiner's equilibrium curvature diagnostic.

### Weakest Paragraph 2 (line 476)

> Proposition \ref{prop:ideal-gas} is the flat baseline of the dictionary. The reading of flatness is that the ideal gas has no microscopic interactions to diagnose: its entropy is additive in the extensive variables and its Ruppeiner geometry is Euclidean in logarithmic coordinates. Interacting models do not share it. For the van der Waals gas with the entropy $S(u, v) = \ln\bigl((v - b)(u + a/v)^{c_v}\bigr)$ per particle in the units $a = b = N = k = 1$ and $c_v = 3/2$ (whose noninteracting limit is the ideal gas of Proposition \ref{prop:ideal-gas}), the Ruppeiner scalar curvature computed in Section \ref{sec:computation} is nonzero away from the critical point and grows toward it: $R = 0.128$ at $(u, v) = (2, 2)$, $R = 0.417$ at $(1, 3)$, and along the line $v = 3$ approaching the critical internal energy $u_c = 0.111$ the values rise to $0.856$ at $u = 0.611$, $9.55$ at $u = 0.211$, and $179.4$ at $u = 0.131$. These values use the Ricci convention of Definition \ref{def:ruppeiner}, in which the round two-sphere has $R = +2$; in Ruppeiner's convention they carry the opposite sign, and the corresponding reading of attraction and repulsion is convention-dependent. The reading of the pattern is Ruppeiner's proposal: flatness corresponds to noninteracting constituents, and curvature to interactions, with the critical region a curvature singularity. In the two models computed here that reading holds. As a general principle connecting thermodynamic curvature to microscopic physics it remains a proposed correspondence supported by examples.

- **House-style diagnosis:** Packs four separate claims into 230 words (physical interpretation of ideal-gas flatness, van der Waals curvature data, repetition of Definition 2.9's Ricci sign convention, and scope of Ruppeiner's conjecture). Its central sentence violates *findings stated directly before qualification* with a 46-word prepositional and parenthetical preamble before the subject (`"the Ruppeiner scalar curvature"`), repeats `"The reading of..."` three times, leaves `"it"` ambiguous in `"Interacting models do not share it"`, and confuses coordinate separability with extensivity (`"additive in the extensive variables"`).

### Weakest Paragraph 3 (line 546)

> What a derivation of gravity from information would require is now fairly easy to state. It would need an assignment of the first row of Table \ref{tab:dictionaries} that is justified independently of gravity, a mechanism that turns the resulting state-space geometry into a spacetime metric, and dynamics that reproduces the Einstein equations in a controlled limit. The first requirement collides with the metric ambiguity: the assignment must choose or derive $f$. The second is what the entropy formula proposes, in its derived special cases. The third is not on record in any setting examined here. Each requirement is a theorem-shaped problem, and the open questions of Section \ref{sec:open} are these three problems in other words.

- **House-style diagnosis:** Opens with cleft throat-clearing filler (`"What a derivation of gravity from information would require is now fairly easy to state. It would need..."`), uses opaque cross-reference shorthand (`"an assignment of the first row of Table \ref{tab:dictionaries}"`), conflates Petz state-space metrics with Ryu–Takayanagi subregion entropies in sentence four, and ends with colloquial filler (`"theorem-shaped problem"`) and inaccurate signposting (`"are these three problems in other words"` for a six-item section).

### Model Rewrite of Paragraph 3 (line 546)

> Deriving gravitational dynamics from quantum state spaces requires three steps that current theorems do not supply. First, a physical principle independent of gravity must select one metric from the Petz family, whereas channel monotonicity leaves an infinite functional parameter $f$ undetermined (Proposition \ref{prop:nonunique}). Second, a construction must map Riemannian geometry on state space to a Lorentzian metric on spacetime; the Ryu--Takayanagi formula instead relates bulk areas to subregion entropies of a fixed state, and its derivations cover only symmetric or semiclassical regimes \cite{chc, lm}. Third, the induced spacetime metric must satisfy Einstein's equations without assuming horizon thermodynamics at the outset \cite{jacobson}.
