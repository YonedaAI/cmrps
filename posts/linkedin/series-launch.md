In algebra, the Classification of Finite Simple Groups answers a deep question definitively: what are the building blocks? Physics has no analogue. Its mathematical representations — algebras, bundles, factors, fields, spacetimes — are studied family by family, with no shared inventory of what makes two systems "the same."

"The Classification of Mathematical Representations of Physical Systems," a twelve-volume program by Matthew Long and The YonedaAI Collaboration, is now complete with a synthesis volume. One ontology of forty-two definitions, one dependency graph, and one equivalence discipline run through every part: each equivalence declares exactly which structure it preserves.

Three kinds of result organize the classification content. Complete invariants appear wherever the invariant is finite or explicitly parameterized: dimension vectors for finite-dimensional C*-algebras, Dynkin and Satake diagrams in semisimple Lie theory, classifying spaces for principal bundles, and Murray–von Neumann and Connes data for amenable factors. Reconstruction theorems — Gelfand, Stone, Doplicher–Roberts — recover objects from categories of probes. Seven obstruction theorems bound the scope of any classification: uncountably many type II₁ factors, algorithmic unrecognizability in dimension four. Across the series, 280 claims are machine-checked in Lean.

Volume 4 states the Yoneda classification principle: an object is fixed up to isomorphism by its relational profile, and representability carries real content in settings with channel-state duality. The program's theorem target is now explicit — the structural classification conjecture, stated with five proof obligations. A taxonomy for physical structure, with proved boundaries, is a concrete place to push.
