🧩 What if we could classify physical theories the way chemists sort the elements — or mathematicians classify the finite simple groups?

That question drives a just-completed twelve-volume research series: “The Classification of Mathematical Representations of Physical Systems,” by Matthew Long and The YonedaAI Collaboration.

🔬 THE PROBLEM
Physics studies structures family by family — algebras, bundles, quantum fields, spacetime — but never had a shared answer to a basic question: when are two systems really the same?

💡 THE INSIGHT
One ontology of 42 definitions runs through all 12 volumes, plus a synthesis volume that ties them together. Where a complete invariant exists, the series finds it: dimension vectors for finite-dimensional operator algebras, Dynkin and Satake diagrams for semisimple Lie theory, classifying spaces for principal bundles, and Murray–von Neumann plus Connes data for amenable factors.

Where classification is impossible, the series proves that too. Seven obstruction theorems draw the line — including uncountably many type II₁ factors, and dimension four, where recognition is algorithmically out of reach.

280 of its claims are machine-checked in Lean.

🌍 WHY IT MATTERS
This is a first full draft of a periodic table for physical structure, with a stated conjecture and five proof obligations showing exactly what comes next.
